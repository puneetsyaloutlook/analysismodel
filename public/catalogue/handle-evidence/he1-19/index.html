    HE1-19 Bibliometric analysis (citation analysis, science mapping, co-citation, co-word analysis)

    Counts and maps publications, authors and citations to show the structure and growth of a field and produces performance tables and network maps.

    Handle evidenceEvidence: UnconfirmedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Counts and maps publications, authors and citations to show the structure and growth of a field. Artefact: performance tables and network maps; read clusters and trends, not single counts.

    When to use it

    Use when you want to know who publishes on a topic, how it has grown and which clusters of work exist.

    When it does not fit

    The topic is small, the database covers the field poorly, or citation counts are read as quality.

    Result format

    The result has performance tables of publications, authors and citations, and network maps of the field. It describes the clusters and trends shown by the maps and does not rest on single counts.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureMiscounts records, merges authors who share a name, and reads citation counts as a measure of quality.
    Error sourcesinput data; data transformation; source selection
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops with poor database coverage of the field (indirectness), name ambiguity, duplicated records and citation lags (risk of bias).

    Related techniques

    • should follow PE-03 Near-duplicate detection (shingling; MinHash; locality-sensitive hashing; text reuse detection): duplicate publication records inflate counts
    • pairs with DM-21 Social network analysis (sociogram, network analysis) Also serves: Assess actors, options and risks.: network structure of authors or citations can be examined with social network analysis
