    HE-06 Opt-in panel respondent quality check (bogus respondent screen)

    Looks for implausible answers that show careless or fraudulent respondents inflating rare responses in an opt-in survey and produces a judgement of inflation risk.

    Handle evidenceEvidence: DocumentedPhase: verify and prepareClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Looks for implausible answers that show careless or fraudulent respondents inflating rare responses. Artefact: a judgement of inflation risk. Read results for under-30 and Hispanic subgroups in opt-in samples first, the subgroups Pew found affected.

    When to use it

    Use when an opt-in online survey reports a rare behaviour or a result for a young or minority subgroup that looks too high.

    When it does not fit

    Fits opt-in online surveys. Pew found few implausible answers in probability panels. Its finding comes from one study of six samples and may not apply to business panels.

    Output specification

    The result is a judgement of the risk that careless or fraudulent respondents have inflated rare responses, with the implausible answers that support it. It reports the results for the under-30 and Hispanic subgroups first when the sample is opt-in.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureFlags real but unusual respondents as bogus, and does not compare rates with a probability sample.
    Error sourcesinput data; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops because the evidence base is one study and results for subgroups are imprecise (imprecision and indirectness).

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. 12 per cent of opt-in respondents under 30 claimed a licence to operate a nuclear submarine, against a true share that rounds to 0 per cent. (Pew Research Center 2024)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
