    Combine findings into conclusions

    These techniques work on the findings, not on your material. They set the findings of different techniques side by side, follow a thread from one to another, weigh several lines of evidence or summarise effects without pooling them. Step 7 chooses from them after the fixed agreement table and narrative summary have run.

    9 techniques. Open one to see what it does, when it fits and what it needs from your material.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    CF-01Mixed methods matrix (case by data source matrix; extension of the Miles and Huberman meta-matrix)Puts the cases that have both qualitative and quantitative data in rows and the data on each case in columns.Documented
    CF-02Following a thread (Moran-Ellis and colleagues)Analyses each component for its key themes and questions, then selects a question or theme from one component and follows it across the other components.Documented
    CF-03Synthesis without meta-analysis reporting (SWiM (Synthesis Without Meta-analysis) guideline)Reports a synthesis of quantitative effects that are not pooled against nine items: grouping of studies, standardised metric, synthesis method, criteria for prioritising results, investigation of heterogeneity, certainty of evidence, data presentation, reporting of results and limitations.Documented
    CF-04Weight of evidence assessment (EFSA (European Food Safety Authority) assemble, weigh and integrate; US EPA weight of evidence)Assembles lines of evidence of different kinds, weighs each for reliability and relevance and integrates them into one conclusion with an uncertainty statement.Documented
    CF-05Non-pooling summaries of effects (vote counting by direction of effect with a sign test; combining P values; summarising effect estimates)Counts studies by the direction of their effect and tests the split with a sign test, combines P values, or reports the median and range of the estimates.Documented
    CF-06Best evidence synthesis (Slavin)Keeps only the best designed studies on a question under explicit inclusion criteria and describes them in detail, with effect sizes where available.Documented
    CF-07Metainference (meta-inference in mixed methods; successive integration of claims)Draws the overall conclusion by integrating the inferences of the qualitative and quantitative strands.Documented
    CF-08Assurance case (Goal Structuring Notation; claims, arguments and evidence)States a top claim, breaks it through argument strategies into sub-claims and ends each branch in a cited item of evidence, with context and assumptions recorded.Documented
    CF-09Nominal group technique and RAND/UCLA appropriateness method (NGT; RAND/UCLA (University of California, Los Angeles) appropriateness method)A panel generates items silently, shares them in turn, discusses them and votes privately (NGT), or rates statements in two rounds on a nine point scale with an evidence summary and a meeting (RAND/UCLA).Documented
