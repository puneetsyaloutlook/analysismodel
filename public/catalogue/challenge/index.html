    Challenge

    15 techniques.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    CH-01Key assumptions check (assumptions check)Lists the stated and unstated premises of the current line, challenges each, and produces a refined assumptions register with the conditions that would invalidate each assumption.Documented
    CH-02Strategic assumption surfacing and testing (SAST)Lists each interested group assumptions, rates them, has opposing groups debate them in four stages, and produces a rated assumptions register.Documented
    CH-03Devil's advocacyBuilds the strongest case against the dominant view or plan, starting from its most vulnerable assumptions, and produces a labelled devil advocate finding.Documented
    CH-04Team A and Team BHas two teams each build the best case for a competing view and debate before a jury of peers, and produces competing papers and debate findings.Documented
    CH-05Red team analysis (red team, red cell)Models how an adversary or other actor would decide by reasoning in that actor first-person voice, or examines a plan from outside the planning team, and produces first-person memos predicting decisions.Documented
    CH-06High-impact and low-probability analysis Also serves: Project forward.Works out plausible pathways to an unlikely but consequential event, with triggers and indicators, and produces pathways and an indicator list.Documented
    CH-07What if analysis Also serves: Project forward.Assumes an unexpected event has occurred, explains how it could have come about, and produces scenarios with indicators and consequence assessments.Documented
    CH-08Premortem (prospective hindsight, inversion) Also serves: Project forward.Asks a team to assume the plan has already failed and to write the reasons, and produces a grouped list of failure reasons and draft assumptions.Practice
    CH-09Structured self-critiqueAsks about sources of uncertainty, evidence, gaps, critical assumptions and alternative hypotheses, and produces a written critique of one own analysis.Documented
    CH-10Rapoport's rules (fair restatement of an opposing position) Also serves: Compare.Requires a fair restatement of the opposing position, a list of points of agreement and of what was learned before any rebuttal, and produces a restatement and an agreement list.Documented
    CH-11Steelmanning Also serves: Compare.Builds the strongest version of a position the analyst disagrees with, responds to it, and produces a strengthened opposing argument and a response.Practice
    CH-12Socratic questioning (Paul and Elder's six question types)Asks a sequence of questions that expose unclear terms, hidden assumptions and unsupported claims, and produces a record of questions and answers and a draft assumptions register.Documented
    CH-13Cognitive bias checks and debiasing (including consider-the-opposite)Runs a conclusion through a list of known judgement biases, applies a counter-procedure, and produces a checklist of marked items where disconfirming evidence should be sought.Unconfirmed
    CH-14Structured brainstormingGenerates and clusters many ideas about a focal question before any critique and produces clustered, prioritised ideas.Documented
    CH-15Group process safeguards (everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice, mindguarding)Controls how a group forms views, with each person contributing in turn, leaders withholding early preferences and evaluators rotating, and produces independent individual views.Documented
