    UC-02 Words of estimative probability (Kent scale; ODNI (Office of the Director of National Intelligence) 2015 seven-bin scale; estimative language)

    Uses a fixed vocabulary of likelihood words, each tied to a numeric range, for every probabilistic judgement.

    State confidence and uncertaintyEvidence: TestedConclusion technique, runs in step 7

    What it does

    Uses a fixed vocabulary of likelihood words, each tied to a numeric range, for every probabilistic judgement. Kent's 1964 scale put "probable" at about 75 per cent and "almost certain" at about 93 per cent. Artefact: key judgements that each carry one term. Read the term against the scale's range, not everyday usage.

    When to use it

    Use when each conclusion needs a stated level of confidence or uncertainty.

    When it does not fit

    Fits readers who know the scale and well defined events. Fails near a bin boundary and when readers bring their own meanings. The ICD 203 version of the terms and its rule on combining likelihood and confidence were not read.

    Result format

    The result is key judgements that each carry one term.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureChooses a term by tone, as a polite hedge, without an underlying probability, or mixes terms from two scales.
    Error sourceslanguage model; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsThe statement expresses a judgement about the findings and adds no evidence. Readers can take the same words as different probabilities, and no scale gives a rule for combining its factors.

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. Rounding 888,328 forecasts to the ODNI scale raised the mean Brier score by 0.7 per cent for all forecasters and 6.1 per cent for superforecasters. In a reader study, verbal terms were matched less closely to intended ranges than numbers. (Friedman et al. 2018, International Studies Quarterly; Mandel and Irwin 2021, Judgment and Decision Making; Marchio 2014, Studies in Intelligence)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
