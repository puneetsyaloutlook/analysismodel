    State confidence and uncertainty

    These techniques give a conclusion a stated level of confidence or uncertainty: a fixed vocabulary of likelihood words, a numeric range, a rating of confidence apart from likelihood, or a record of how the conclusion was reached. Step 7 chooses from them. They change how uncertainty is stated and do not change the confidence label.

    12 techniques. Open one to see what it does, when it fits and what it needs from your material.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    UC-01Confidence in qualitative evidence findings (GRADE-CERQual (Confidence in the Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative research))Rates each finding of a qualitative evidence synthesis on four components: methodological limitations, relevance, coherence and adequacy of data.Documented
    UC-02Words of estimative probability (Kent scale; ODNI (Office of the Director of National Intelligence) 2015 seven-bin scale; estimative language)Uses a fixed vocabulary of likelihood words, each tied to a numeric range, for every probabilistic judgement.Tested
    UC-03PHIA probability yardstick (UK Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment yardstick)Ties seven terms to approximate ranges with deliberate gaps between them, from "remote chance" (more than 0 to about 5 per cent) to "almost certain" (about 95 to under 100 per cent); "realistic possibility" is about 40 to under 50 per cent.Documented
    UC-04Analytical confidence rating stated apart from likelihood (PHIA analytical confidence; NIC (National Intelligence Council) "What we mean when we say" box; key judgements with separate likelihood and confidence)Rates confidence in each judgement as high, moderate or low from the information base, analytical rigour, and complexity and volatility, and states it separately from the likelihood term.Documented
    UC-05IPCC calibrated uncertainty language (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change likelihood scale and confidence from evidence and agreement)Uses two metrics.Tested
    UC-06Traceable account (IPCC traceable account of evidence and agreement)Records in the text how evidence and agreement were evaluated to reach the stated confidence or likelihood.Documented
    UC-07Verbal term with numeric range in the same sentence (dual expression; numeric-bounded verbal probability)Follows each verbal probability term with its numeric range, for example "very likely (greater than 90 per cent)".Tested
    UC-08Numeric probability statement (point probability or probability range without verbal labels)States the probability as a number or numeric range with no verbal term.Tested
    UC-09Point estimate with numeric range (estimate plus interval, for example "about 500 (range 400 to 600)")States a quantity as a best estimate and a range.Tested
    UC-10Certainty-graded effect statements (GRADE informative statements (GRADE guidelines 26))Combines the certainty of evidence with the size of effect in fixed wording: "X reduces outcome" for high certainty, "probably reduces" for moderate, "may reduce" for low and "the evidence is very uncertain" for very low.Documented
    UC-11NUSAP notation and pedigree matrix (numeral, unit, spread, assessment, pedigree (Funtowicz and Ravetz))Reports a number with five qualifiers: numeral, unit, spread, assessment and pedigree, where pedigree scores how the number was produced.Documented
    UC-12Ordered scale of uncertainty expressions (van der Bles framework of direct and indirect uncertainty)Ranks nine ways to express uncertainty about a fact or number, from a full probability distribution to explicit denial, and keeps that direct uncertainty separate from indirect uncertainty about the quality of the evidence.Documented
