    UC-08 Numeric probability statement (point probability or probability range without verbal labels)

    States the probability as a number or numeric range with no verbal term.

    State confidence and uncertaintyEvidence: TestedConclusion technique, runs in step 7

    What it does

    States the probability as a number or numeric range with no verbal term. Artefact: a percentage or range. Read the bounds.

    When to use it

    Use when each conclusion needs a stated level of confidence or uncertainty.

    When it does not fit

    Fits when transparency and agreement between readers matter more than ease of writing. Fails when the number implies more precision than the evidence carries.

    Result format

    The result is a percentage or range.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureProduces a precise-looking number, such as 73 per cent, with no derivation.
    Error sourceslanguage model; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsThe statement expresses a judgement about the findings and adds no evidence. Readers can take the same words as different probabilities, and no scale gives a rule for combining its factors.

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. In the same study verbal best estimates clustered near 50 per cent, and a fifty-fifty response was given for 37.2 per cent of verbal items against 5.8 per cent of numeric ones. The numeric condition used ranges, and point values were not tested. (Mandel and Irwin 2021, Judgment and Decision Making; Marchio 2014, Studies in Intelligence)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
