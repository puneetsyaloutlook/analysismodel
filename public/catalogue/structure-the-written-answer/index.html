    Structure the written answer

    These techniques decide the order and layers of a written answer: the conclusion first, a short summary above a longer report, a structured abstract, a plain language summary or a policy brief. Step 8 chooses from them to shape how the seven parts of the answer are written.

    8 techniques. Open one to see what it does, when it fits and what it needs from your material.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    SA-01Inverted pyramid (answer first; most important first; front-loading)Puts the main idea (a summary, conclusion, recommendation or required action) first and the supporting detail after it in descending order of importance.Documented
    SA-02Structure type choice (hierarchical, sequential or narrative structure)Chooses the overall structure from the reader's task: hierarchical for scanning by topic, sequential for steps and processes, narrative for the whole account.Documented
    SA-03Structured abstract (structured summary with fixed sub-headings)Writes the summary under fixed sub-headings, in Hartley's version background, aim, method, results and conclusions, with a word cap.Tested
    SA-04Plain language summary with a standard template (Cochrane plain language summary format)Writes a lay summary in a fixed template that states the effect, the quality of the evidence and a table of results.Tested
    SA-051:3:25 layered report (reader-friendly writing (Canadian Health Services Research Foundation, 2001))Writes three layers: one page of main messages, a three page executive summary and 25 pages of findings and method for non-specialists.Documented
    SA-06Policy brief (evidence brief for policy; short evidence summary)Summarises research for policy readers in a short brief (three pages in the tested case), sometimes with an opinion piece.Tested
    SA-07Headings and preview sentences (signalling topic structure)Marks the topic structure of a text with headings or with sentences that announce the topics to come.Tested
    SA-08Pyramid principle (Minto pyramid; governing thought with grouped supporting ideas)Puts a governing thought at the top, groups the supporting ideas beneath it so that each group is summarised by the idea above, and opens with situation, complication, question and answer.Documented
