    Design the display

    These techniques cover how to show a finding as a chart or graphic: which encoding to use, which chart fits the message, how to title and label it, where the axis starts and how to make it accessible. Step 8 chooses from them only when the answer shows numbers or charts.

    15 techniques. Open one to see what it does, when it fits and what it needs from your material.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    VD-01Encoding choice by perceptual accuracy ranking (Cleveland and McGill graphical perception ranking; elementary perceptual tasks)Chooses the visual channel for the comparison the reader must make from an accuracy order: position on a common scale first, then position on unaligned scales, length, direction and angle, area, volume and curvature, with shading and colour saturation last.Tested
    VD-02Expressiveness and effectiveness criteria (Mackinlay; APT (A Presentation Tool))Tests a candidate design twice: that it encodes all and only the facts in the data (expressiveness), and that its perceptual tasks are the most accurate available for each data type (effectiveness).Documented
    VD-03Chart choice by message and relationship (chart chooser)Writes the message in a few sentences, names its relationship (time series, ranking, correlation, part to whole) and picks the chart type matched to it, such as a line chart for a time series.Documented
    VD-04Message headline title with statistical subtitle (active title)Writes the finding as the chart title and what, where and when the data cover as the subtitle, both in body text rather than in the image.Documented
    VD-05Direct labelling instead of legendsPlaces category names on or next to the lines, bars or segments they name.Documented
    VD-06Annotation (annotated chart; messaging)Adds short text close to features of the data to highlight them and give context.Documented
    VD-07Small multiples (panel charts; trellis display; faceting)Splits one crowded chart into a grid of small charts, one per category, with the same y-axis scale.Documented
    VD-08Focus chart (highlight and grey context)Colours the series of interest and draws the others in light grey.Documented
    VD-09Axis baseline rules (zero baseline for bars; marked breaks for lines; dot plot alternative)Starts bars at zero on an unbroken axis, allows a break on a line chart only if it is marked and described, and replaces bars with a Cleveland dot plot where zero hides the story.Tested
    VD-10Separate panels instead of dual axesPlots two measures with different units in separate charts stacked on one x-axis, each with its own y-axis, rather than on two y-axes of one chart.Documented
    VD-11Category and clutter limits (four lines, four stacks, five pie segments; no 3D or chart junk)Caps the categories per chart type (pies five or fewer, at most four categories per stacked bar and four bars per cluster, about four lines), combines minor categories and removes 3D, patterns, shadows and heavy gridlines.Documented
    VD-12Gridline and chart size settings (Heer and Bostock)Sets chart height at 80 pixels or more and adds light gridlines (alpha about 0.2) spaced at least 8 pixels apart.Tested
    VD-13Narrative visualisation structures (martini glass; interactive slideshow; drill-down story; Segel and Heer genres)Arranges charts and text into an ordered story on a spectrum from author-driven (linear, heavy messaging) to reader-driven (free order and interaction).Documented
    VD-14Infographic summary (visual summary of a review)Presents the summary of a review as a one-page graphic instead of text.Tested
    VD-15Accessible chart publishing (text alternative; data table; no colour-only encoding; dashboard headline first)Pairs every chart with a text description of its message or a data table, keeps titles and notes as text, avoids matching by colour alone and gives an accessible download.Documented
