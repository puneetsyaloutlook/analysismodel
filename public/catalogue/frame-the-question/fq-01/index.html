    FQ-01 PICO family (population, intervention, comparison, outcome; variants PICOT, PEO, PICo, SPIDER)

    Splits a vague topic into named slots (population, intervention or exposure, comparison, outcome) and produces a one-sentence structured question and a slot table.

    Frame the questionEvidence: DocumentedQuestion technique, runs in step 5.1Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Splits a vague topic into named slots. Artefact: a one-sentence structured question and a slot table. An empty or vague slot shows the question is not yet researchable. PICOT adds type of study; PEO (population, exposure, outcome) replaces the intervention with an exposure; PICo (population, phenomenon of interest, context) and SPIDER (sample, phenomenon of interest, design, evaluation, research type) suit qualitative questions.

    When to use it

    Use when the topic is vague and you need a researchable question about an intervention, an exposure or an experience.

    When it does not fit

    No comparator exists or the outcome cannot be observed (switch to PEO, PICo or SPIDER). Designed for health and social science and not tested for policy or business topics.

    Result format

    The result gives a one-sentence structured question and a slot table with one row per slot (population, intervention or exposure, comparison, outcome, or the slots of the variant used), each row holding the content of that slot. It marks any slot that is empty or vague and states whether that means the question is not yet researchable.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureFills an empty slot with a plausible invention instead of reporting that the slot cannot be filled from the topic.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; language model
    Certainty limitsIndirectness if the topic is not about an intervention, exposure or experience; the technique shapes the question and does not test any claim, so certainty depends on later techniques.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with FQ-03 Definitional analysis (explication, concept clarification, conceptual analysis): Working definitions for each slot term remove ambiguity inside the slots
    • pairs with FQ-06 SMART check (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound): Criteria checking confirms the structured question has a measure and a time limit

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
