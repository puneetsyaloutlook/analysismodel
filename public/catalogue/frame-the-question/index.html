    Frame the question

    These techniques work on your request, not on your material. They split a vague topic into named parts, start from the decision to be made, replace ambiguous terms with working definitions, or read the topic through several models. They run first, in step 5.1, and show you what they found so that you can keep or edit the request.

    6 techniques. Open one to see what it does, when it fits and what it needs from your material.

    IDTechniquePurposeEvidence status
    FQ-01PICO family (population, intervention, comparison, outcome; variants PICOT, PEO, PICo, SPIDER)Splits a vague topic into named slots (population, intervention or exposure, comparison, outcome) and produces a one-sentence structured question and a slot table.Documented
    FQ-02Decision-first framing (decision-focused or value-focused question design)Starts from the decision to be made and works back to a list of questions, each tagged with the option it could change.Documented
    FQ-03Definitional analysis (explication, concept clarification, conceptual analysis)Replaces ambiguous key terms with precise working definitions and produces a glossary with borderline cases.Documented
    FQ-04Reframing and issue redefinition (changing the level of analysis or baseline; five-step issue redefinition)Restates the question at another level, with another baseline, or by rephrasing, broadening, narrowing and reversing it, and produces answers side by side.Practice
    FQ-05Multiple mental models (applying explicit models from several fields to one topic)Reads one topic through several explicit models from different fields and produces one reading per model.Documented
    FQ-06SMART check (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound)Checks that an objective or question names a scope, a measure, a feasible target and a time limit, and produces the draft rewritten with each criterion answered.Documented
