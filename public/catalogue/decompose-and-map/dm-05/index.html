    DM-05 Taxonomy building (classification scheme, typology, controlled vocabulary)

    Builds a scheme of classes from observable characteristics, assigns items to the classes and produces a hierarchy and a draft codebook.

    Decompose and mapEvidence: DocumentedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Builds a scheme of classes and assigns items to them. Artefact: usually a hierarchy; read the spread of items across classes and items that fit no class.

    When to use it

    Use when you have a set of items and need a class scheme that sorts them and shows which items fit no class.

    When it does not fit

    Items are often listed under several parents, many are unclassifiable, or classes cannot be told apart, which shows the chosen characteristics are wrong.

    Output specification

    The result is a classification scheme, usually a hierarchy, with each class named and each item assigned to a class. It reports the spread of items across classes and lists the items that fit no class.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureInvents classes from the item labels alone and assigns items to them without checking class overlap.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; language model
    Certainty limitsRisk of bias from the choice of characteristics; unclassified and multiply classified items lower certainty by indirectness.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with DM-06 Affinity diagram (KJ method): Affinity grouping suggests classes from the items before the scheme is fixed
    • pairs with HE1-15 Content analysis (conventional, directed and summative): Content analysis applies a fixed category scheme to text and counts use

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Taxonomies rely on empirical characteristics and typologies on abstract or subjective criteria. A taxonomy development technique from information systems research was not opened. (Wikipedia, Taxonomy (general))

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
