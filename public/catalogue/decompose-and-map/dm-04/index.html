    DM-04 Value chain analysis (Porter)

    Divides the activities of one business unit into five primary and four support activities and produces a value chain showing where cost and differentiation concentrate.

    Decompose and mapEvidence: DocumentedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Divides a business unit's activities into five primary and four support activities. Artefact: a chain; read where cost and differentiation concentrate.

    When to use it

    Use when you want to see which activities of one business unit create cost or differentiation.

    When it does not fit

    Activities fit no listed category, as in services or platforms, which shows the manufacturing-derived structure is wrong. Porter advises business-unit level because higher levels hide advantage.

    Output specification

    The result is a chain of the business unit's five primary activities and four support activities, with what the unit does in each activity. It names where cost and differentiation concentrate along the chain.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel alone
    Typical model failureAssigns activities to the nine categories from general knowledge and invents activity-level costs that are not published.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; language model
    Certainty limitsImprecision because activity-level costs are seldom published; indirectness when the unit is a service or platform.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with DM-17 Process mapping (flowcharts, swimlane diagrams, value stream maps): Process mapping gives the activity sequence that the chain summarises
    • pairs with CM1-12 Benchmarking and peer comparison (best-practice comparison, competitor analysis): Benchmarking compares activity costs with peers

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Described on a secondary page citing Porter (1985); the page reports no criticisms, and criticisms for services and networks are unconfirmed. (Wikipedia, Value chain)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
