    CM1-06 Case selection techniques (extreme, deviant, diverse, index, most similar)

    Chooses which cases to compare by their position on a variable distribution or on a model prediction, producing a chosen case set with the rule used.

    CompareEvidence: UnconfirmedPreparation technique, runs in step 5.2Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Chooses which cases to compare by their position on a variable's distribution or on a model's prediction. Artefact: a chosen case set.

    When to use it

    Use when a user has many possible cases and needs a defensible way to pick a few to study, such as extreme, typical or deviant ones.

    When it does not fit

    Not established; the 2022 chapter positions these techniques as exploratory, not confirmatory.

    Result format

    The result is the chosen set of cases, each named with its type (extreme, deviant, diverse, index or most similar) and its position on the variable's distribution or on the model's prediction that led to its selection.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureSelects extreme cases on the outcome and presents them as typical, or applies a selection rule the source does not define.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops because the definitions of the five techniques could not be retrieved, and because selection on the outcome limits any causal reading (risk of bias).

    Related techniques

    • should follow MM-01 Exploratory data analysis with plots and distribution checks (EDA (exploratory data analysis); summary statistics; Q-Q plot; scatterplot matrix): the shape of the selection variable should be seen before cases are chosen by position on it
    • pairs with CM1-05 Comparative case study (structured focused comparison, small-N comparison): the chosen cases are then compared in depth
