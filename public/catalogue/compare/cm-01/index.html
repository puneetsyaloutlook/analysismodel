    4. CM-01 Sponsor versus non-sponsor comparison (meta-epidemiological sponsorship analysis)

    CM-01 Sponsor versus non-sponsor comparison (meta-epidemiological sponsorship analysis)

    Compares the share of favourable results and conclusions between sponsored and independently funded reports on one topic, producing a risk ratio or odds ratio.

    CompareEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: show a link between things

    What it does

    Compares the share of favourable results and conclusions between sponsored and independently funded reports on one topic. Artefact: a risk ratio or odds ratio. In health research the risk ratios were 1.27 for favourable efficacy results and 1.34 for favourable conclusions.

    When to use it

    Use when a user asks whether industry or sponsor funding is linked to more favourable findings in the reports on a topic.

    When it does not fit

    Needs enough independent reports on one question. The health studies are observational, so the result is an association. The harms result was not significant. No study of surveys or consultancy reports was found, so applying the result to them is an inference.

    Result format

    The result gives the number of sponsored and independently funded reports on the topic and the share of favourable results and favourable conclusions in each group. It reports a risk ratio or odds ratio for each of results and conclusions, and ends with a statement of what the ratio shows about sponsorship.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureAssigns a sponsor from the logo alone and treats independence as proof of quality.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops because the evidence is observational (risk of bias), when few reports fall in one group (imprecision), and when the result is carried from health studies to other fields (indirectness).

    Related techniques

    • pairs with HE1-02 Quality of information check: a per-source quality rating covers accuracy, which a funding comparison does not
    • should follow HE1-08 Systematic review: a systematic search supplies a defensible set of reports to compare

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. A Cochrane review of 75 papers found the pattern was not explained by standard risk of bias items. An older review of 30 studies gave an odds ratio of 4.05. (Lundh et al. 2017, Cochrane abstract; Cochrane summary page, industry sponsorship and research outcome; Pharmaceutical industry sponsorship and research outcome and quality)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
