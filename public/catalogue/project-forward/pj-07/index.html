    PJ-07 Technology S-curve analysis (logistic growth, technology substitution curve)

    Fit a logistic curve to an adoption or performance series to estimate a ceiling and an inflection date, as a draft result.

    Project forwardEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: predict, without claiming a cause

    What it does

    Fits a slow, fast and saturating curve to adoption or performance. Artefact: a fitted curve with ceiling and inflection date.

    When to use it

    Use when you ask how far or how fast adoption or performance will grow and the series has passed its inflection point or a ceiling is known.

    When it does not fit

    It is fitted before the inflection point (the ceiling is then poorly determined), the metric changes meaning, or the technology is replaced instead of saturating.

    Result format

    The result is a fitted logistic curve for adoption or performance, showing the slow, fast and saturating phases. It states the ceiling and the inflection date.

    Method steps

    1. Choose the metric: adoption share, cumulative units or a performance measure. State the ceiling it could reach, if the material gives one.
    2. Collect the time series and note how much of the curve it covers. A series that covers only the early part gives a wide range of fits.
    3. Check the conditions for an S-curve: growth that feeds on itself and a limit, such as a finite market or competition for a limited resource.
    4. Fit a logistic curve (or another S-shaped form such as Gompertz). Record the ceiling, the inflection date and the growth rate.
    5. Give the range of ceilings and inflection dates that fit the data about equally well.
    6. Write the result: the fitted curve, the ceiling and inflection date with their ranges, and the share of the curve the data covers.

    Steps drawn from: Modis, Strengths and weaknesses of S-curves, Technological Forecasting and Social Change 2007 and Stevens Institute, forecasting technological substitutions.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureReports a ceiling from a fit made before the inflection point as if it were well determined.
    Error sourcesinput data; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the fit starts before the inflection point (imprecision), the ceiling is assumed (risk of bias), or the metric changes meaning (indirectness).

    Related techniques

    • pairs with PJ-01 Trend extrapolation and time-series forecasting (exponential smoothing, autoregressive integrated moving average models, trend impact analysis): plain extrapolation gives a comparison when the ceiling is uncertain
    • should follow PE-04 Data profiling and rule-based validation (data audit; data quality assessment; edit checks): a data quality report shows changes in how the metric is measured

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Logistic and other S-shaped curves (Gompertz, Fisher-Pry, Weibull) are widely used to forecast technological substitution and adoption. Modis sets out when an S-curve can be used and how fitting and its uncertainties work. (Modis 2007, Technological Forecasting and Social Change; Stevens Institute abstract)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
