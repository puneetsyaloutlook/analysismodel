    PJ-24 Weak signal detection (early warning signs, wild cards)

    Identify small ambiguous early indications that may precede a discontinuity, giving a watchlist with a strength rating over time as an appraised evidence base.

    Project forwardEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Identifies small ambiguous early indications that may precede a discontinuity. Artefact: a watchlist with strength rating over time; check whether a signal is gaining independent sources.

    When to use it

    Use when you are watching a defined domain for faint early indications and want to see whether any is gaining independent sources.

    When it does not fit

    Signals are chosen after the event, or signals that did not materialise are not recorded, so the hit rate cannot be known.

    Result format

    The result is a watchlist with one row per weak signal, giving the signal and its strength rating over time. It states for each signal whether it is gaining independent sources.

    Method steps

    1. State the area to scan and the time frame the material covers.
    2. Scan the material for items that are new, ambiguous or odd for the area, and that seem small now. List each as a candidate signal, with its source and date.
    3. For each candidate, state what has already happened, and what bigger change it could be an early sign of.
    4. For each, ask which alternative futures it opens, and whether it could be a counter-trend to a known trend.
    5. Rate the strength of each signal (number of independent sources, and growth over time where the material has dates).
    6. Write the result: a watchlist of signals with source, date, strength and the change each may point to. Mark which have more than one independent source.

    Steps drawn from: Sitra, Weak signals from the future (preparation, scanning, interpretation) and the Government of Canada foresight training manual, module 3. Rating over time needs dated material.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureSelects signals after an event occurs and omits signals that did not materialise.
    Error sourcessource selection; expert opinion; language model
    Certainty limitsConfidence is low because no study shows how often weak signals precede events (indirectness), and unrecorded misses inflate the apparent hit rate (reporting bias).

    Related techniques

    • should follow PJ-23 Horizon scanning (environmental scanning): a scan produces the signal log that is rated here
    • pairs with IS-06 Source dependence and copy detection (source independence analysis): copy detection tests whether a strengthening signal has independent sources

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Ansoff introduced weak signals in 1975, and Hiltunen defines a weak signal as the first symptom of a change that may be significant later. Sitra's guide divides the work into preparation, scanning and interpretation. (Sitra, Weak signals from the future; Government of Canada, foresight training manual; Springer, Sustainability Science 2025)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
