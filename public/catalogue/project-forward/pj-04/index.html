    PJ-04 Delphi technique (real-time Delphi, mini-Delphi)

    Collect anonymous expert estimates over two or more rounds with controlled feedback, giving the median and interquartile range for each round as a draft result.

    Project forwardEvidence: DocumentedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: predict without implying cause

    What it does

    Collects anonymous expert estimates over two or more rounds with controlled feedback. Artefact: median and interquartile range per round; treat persistent disagreement as information.

    When to use it

    Use when no usable data exist and you need a structured, anonymous view from a panel of experts on a quantity or event.

    When it does not fit

    The panel is a convenience sample with similar views, no feedback is given between rounds, or consensus is reported as accuracy.

    Output specification

    The result is a table with one row per round giving the median and interquartile range of the expert estimates. It names any disagreement that persists across rounds and treats it as information.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureSimulates the experts itself and reports a consensus without any real panel or feedback rounds.
    Error sourcesexpert opinion; source selection
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the panel is small or homogeneous (risk of bias), when disagreement persists (inconsistency), or when validation studies are few (indirectness).

    Related techniques

    • pairs with TC1-14 Coherence correction and averaging of probability judgements: coherence correction and averaging of the panel probabilities
    • pairs with PJ-06 Structured expert elicitation (IDEA protocol: investigate, discuss, estimate, aggregate): calibration questions and ranges from structured elicitation reduce overconfident point estimates

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Rowe and Wright (1999) report few validation studies, often without a description of conditions, so it is hard to identify which aspects relate to accuracy. The UK Futures Toolkit lists it, which shows use, not accuracy. (Rowe and Wright (1999), Delphi, abstract page; GOV.UK Futures Toolkit case study)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
