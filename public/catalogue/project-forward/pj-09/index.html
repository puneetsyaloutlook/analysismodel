    PJ-09 Hype cycle (technology expectations curve)

    Place a technology on a five-phase expectations curve with a years-to-mainstream band, giving a placement to compare with measured attention data as a draft result.

    Project forwardEvidence: TestedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: predict without implying cause

    What it does

    Positions a technology on a five-phase expectations curve with a years-to-mainstream label. Artefact: a placement and time band, to be compared with measured attention data.

    When to use it

    Use when you want a rough view of where a commercial information technology sits in its attention cycle and how far it is from mainstream use.

    When it does not fit

    The attention series shows no rise, fall and partial recovery, the plateau label is applied without adoption data, or the technology is outside the commercial information technology setting it was designed for.

    Output specification

    The result is a placement of the technology in one of the five phases of the expectations curve, with a years-to-mainstream time band. It compares the placement with measured attention data.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failurePlaces a technology on the curve from general impression without any attention or adoption series.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops because published hit rates for placements are modest (indirectness), proxies for attention are weak (imprecision), and placement is a judgement (risk of bias).

    Related techniques

    • pairs with PJ-07 Technology S-curve analysis (logistic growth, technology substitution curve): a measured adoption curve checks the placement
    • should follow PJ-23 Horizon scanning (environmental scanning): scanned signals supply the attention evidence for the placement

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. A peer-reviewed review using Gartner's own databases reports incongruences. A vendor analysis (not peer reviewed) reports a 60 per cent hit rate on 403 placements, falling to 45 per cent for 5 to 10 year horizons; its figures are an unreviewed claim. (Dedehayir and Steinert (2016), review of the hype cycle, abstract page; Envisioning, hype cycle findings (vendor analysis))

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
