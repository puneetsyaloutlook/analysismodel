    PJ-22 Technology roadmapping

    Lay out how markets, products, technologies and resources are expected to evolve on a common time axis, giving a layered time chart with dependencies as a draft result.

    Project forwardEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Lays out how markets, products, technologies and resources are expected to evolve on a common time axis. Artefact: a layered time chart with dependencies; read milestones with no technology or resource milestone behind them.

    When to use it

    Use when you need to coordinate a plan across product, technology and resource layers and see what each milestone depends on.

    When it does not fit

    Dates are shown without owners or dependencies, or a plan is treated as a forecast.

    Result format

    The result is a layered chart on a common time axis, with separate layers for markets, products, technologies and resources and the dependencies between them marked. It names the milestones that have no technology or resource milestone behind them.

    Method steps

    1. State the focus of the roadmap and the time frames: now, short term, medium term and long term.
    2. Make one row each for market and business drivers, product or service, technology, and enablers or resources.
    3. Fill the market and business drivers row: what changes the sector and the business over the time frames.
    4. Fill the product or service row: the product features or targets needed at each time frame to meet those drivers.
    5. Fill the technology row: the technology solutions that would deliver each product feature, and when. Add the enablers and resources they need.
    6. Draw links between items in different rows. Mark gaps: a driver with no product response, or a feature with no technology.
    7. Write the result: the roadmap as a table of rows by time frame, with the links and gaps listed.

    Steps drawn from: the T-Plan process and product-technology roadmap canvas of the University of Cambridge Institute for Manufacturing (Phaal, Roadmapping for strategy and innovation; roadmap template).

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureShows dates with no owners or dependencies and treats announced dates as forecasts.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsConfidence drops when dates come from announcements with no delivery record (indirectness) or when dependencies are not tied to a named owner (inconsistency).

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Technology roadmapping began at Motorola in the 1970s. The Cambridge Institute for Manufacturing describes a multi-layered, time-based chart that links market needs, product plans and technology, and a workshop process (T-Plan) for building it. (Phaal, Roadmapping for strategy and innovation; IfM, T-Plan)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
