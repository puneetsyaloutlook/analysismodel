    MM-08 LLM coding and zero-shot classification of text (LLM annotation; LLM-assisted deductive coding; few-shot classification; natural language inference classification)

    Apply a human-written codebook or category descriptions to text with a large language model and produce codes, rationales and per-code agreement with human coders.

    Measure and modelEvidence: TestedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Applies a human-written codebook, or category descriptions, to text with a large language model (LLM). Artefact: codes, rationales and per-code agreement with human coders. Read the failing codes and the disagreement cases.

    When to use it

    Use when you have a written codebook and a body of text too large to code by hand and want a model to apply the codes.

    When it does not fit

    Pilot on a human-coded sample, and require per-code agreement, repeated-run stability and error analysis before scaling. Misfit: agreement checked only overall, one prompt tried, or error patterns by group not examined.

    Output specification

    The result is a table with one row per document, giving the code assigned and the rationale, and a per-code agreement figure with human coders. It lists the codes that fail and the cases where the model and the human coders disagree.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureCodes whole sets confidently without a validation sample, returns different labels on a rerun, and gives plausible rationales that do not justify the label.
    Error sourcesinput data; language model; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when agreement is low on any code (risk of bias), when labels change across runs or prompts (inconsistency) and when errors differ by subject group (indirectness).

    Related techniques

    • should follow CK-04 Gold-standard validation sample (ground truth set; human-coded validation set): a human-coded sample is needed for the pilot
    • pairs with CK-02 Model versus human coding agreement protocol (LLM-as-coder reliability test; intra-coder agreement across runs): agreement against human coders and across runs
    • pairs with CK-10 Repeated-run consistency test (test-retest; intra-coder reliability; TARr@N and TARa@N (raw and parsed answer agreement); settings pinning): repeated runs show stability
    • pairs with CK-12 Specification sweep across models and prompts (LLM hacking test; multiverse analysis; specification curve): a specification sweep shows whether conclusions depend on prompt and model
    • pairs with CK-06 Bias correction of model labels with a human-coded subsample (design-based supervised learning (DSL); prediction-powered inference (PPI); regression estimator correction): bias correction is needed when labels are used in a numeric claim
    • substitutes for HE1-15 Content analysis (conventional, directed and summative): when a fixed codebook exists and the volume class is needs automation

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. Studies from 2023 to 2025 disagree by task. ChatGPT beat crowd workers on four of five tasks on 2,382 tweets. GPT-4 reached kappa of 0.79 or more on 3 of 9 codes and 0.6 or more on 8 of 9 in one case study. Across 13 million labels from 18 models, about 31 per cent of hypotheses reached incorrect conclusions. Errors on interview transcripts were not random with respect to subject characteristics. (Gilardi et al., arXiv 2303.15056; Dunivin, arXiv 2401.15170; Baumann et al. 2025, arXiv 2509.08825; Ashwin et al., arXiv 2309.17147; Ziems et al., arXiv 2305.03514; Pangakis et al., arXiv 2306.00176; Reiss, arXiv 2304.11085; Bano et al., arXiv 2306.13298; Tai et al., DOAJ record; Törnberg, arXiv 2304.06588)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
