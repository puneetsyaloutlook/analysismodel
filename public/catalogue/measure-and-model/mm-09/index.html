    MM-09 Sentiment analysis (polarity detection; opinion mining; affect lexicon)

    Score text as positive, negative or neutral, or by emotion, and produce scores per document with aggregates by group or period.

    Measure and modelEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Scores text as positive, negative or neutral, or by emotion. Artefact: scores per document and aggregates by group or period. Read the distribution and a sample of high and low scores.

    When to use it

    Use when tone or emotion in text is itself the quantity you want to compare across groups or periods.

    When it does not fit

    Fits when tone is the quantity of interest. Misfit: controlled language in policy documents and filings, or negation and sarcasm that reverse a score. Off-the-shelf lexicons need validation in the domain.

    Result format

    The result has a sentiment score for each document and aggregates by group or period. It shows the distribution of scores and a sample of high-scoring and low-scoring documents.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel plus code
    Typical model failureReports tone as if it were position.
    Error sourcesinput data; data transformation; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the lexicon or model was built for another domain (indirectness) and when scores are not checked against human ratings (risk of bias).

    Related techniques

    • should follow PE-02 Preprocessing sensitivity testing (text cleaning; tokenisation; stopword removal; stemming; boilerplate removal; preText): cleaning choices change scores
    • pairs with CK-08 Semantic, predictive and convergent validity checks (construct validity checks): validity checks test whether scores behave as tone
    • pairs with MM-10 Stance detection (position detection): tone is not stance, so stance detection answers the position question

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. A text can be negative in wording and still support its target. (Mohammad et al. 2016, arXiv 1605.01655; Grimmer and Stewart 2013, Political Analysis)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
