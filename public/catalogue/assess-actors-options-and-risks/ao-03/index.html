    AO-03 Stakeholder salience model (Mitchell, Agle and Wood, 1997)

    Ranks actors by legitimacy, power and urgency, producing a diagram with eight classes from definitive to non-stakeholder.

    Assess actors, options and risksEvidence: DocumentedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Ranks stakeholders by legitimacy, power and urgency. Artefact: a diagram with eight classes (definitive, dominant, dangerous, dependent, dormant, demanding, discretionary, non-stakeholder).

    When to use it

    Use when a user asks which actors deserve attention first once their legitimacy and the urgency of their claims are taken into account.

    When it does not fit

    Attribute ratings cannot be evidenced.

    Output specification

    The result is a diagram or table that assigns each stakeholder to one of eight classes: definitive, dominant, dangerous, dependent, dormant, demanding, discretionary or non-stakeholder. Each assignment shows the stakeholder's legitimacy, power and urgency, and the stakeholders are ranked by salience.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureRates urgency and legitimacy from the model general knowledge of an organisation and not from evidence about the actor in this case.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; language model
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when attribute ratings have no evidence behind them (risk of bias) and when the situation changes the urgency of claims (indirectness).
