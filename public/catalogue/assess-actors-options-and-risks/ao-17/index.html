    AO-17 Bow-tie analysis

    Maps causes, a central event, consequences and the barriers on each path, producing a cause, event and consequence diagram that shows pathways lacking a barrier.

    Assess actors, options and risksEvidence: DocumentedAnalysis technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Maps causes, a central event, consequences and the barriers on each path. Artefact: a cause, event and consequence diagram; read pathways that lack a barrier.

    When to use it

    Use when a user asks whether the controls against a specific unwanted event cover every cause and consequence.

    When it does not fit

    A quantified probability is needed; bow-ties are typically qualitative and less rigorous than fault tree analysis.

    Result format

    The result is a diagram with the central event in the middle, the causes on one side and the consequences on the other, and the barriers marked on each path. It names the pathways that lack a barrier.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureLists generic barriers for each path without checking that they are independent or actually in place.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the existence and independence of barriers are assumed (risk of bias), and when only one source described the event.
