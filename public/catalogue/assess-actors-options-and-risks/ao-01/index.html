    AO-01 Stakeholder analysis (stakeholder mapping)

    Identifies who is affected by or can affect a topic and classifies each by interest, influence and attitude, producing a stakeholder register and grid.

    Assess actors, options and risksEvidence: DocumentedAnalysis technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Identifies who is affected by or can affect a topic and classifies them by interest, influence and attitude. Artefact: a stakeholder register and grid; read who may block or support and who is negative.

    When to use it

    Use when a user asks who will support or block a proposal, or who needs to be consulted.

    When it does not fit

    The list is longer than can be mapped or acted on.

    Result format

    The result is a stakeholder register with one row per stakeholder, giving their interest, influence and attitude, and a grid that places each stakeholder by those classifications. It names the stakeholders who may block, those who may support and those who are negative.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureLists every possible party without limiting the list, and rates interest and influence from role titles instead of evidence.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when ratings rest on judgement without published evidence (risk of bias), when positions have changed since the evidence was written (indirectness), and when internal actors are not visible.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with DM-21 Social network analysis (sociogram, network analysis) Also serves: Assess actors, options and risks.: network measures find influence through ties that role-based ratings miss
    • should follow FQ-02 Decision-first framing (decision-focused or value-focused question design): a decision-first frame limits the list to actors who can change the decision

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. The page cites Eden and Ackermann (2013) that the potential list often exceeds the available time. (Wikipedia, Stakeholder analysis)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
