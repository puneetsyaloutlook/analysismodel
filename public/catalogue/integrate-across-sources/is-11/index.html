    IS-11 Exploratory sequential integration (exploratory sequential design)

    Uses qualitative results to decide what is measured in a later quantitative stage and produces measures or hypotheses derived from themes.

    Integrate across sourcesEvidence: DocumentedPhase: analyseClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Takes qualitative results first and uses them to decide what is measured in a later quantitative stage. Artefact: measures or hypotheses derived from themes. Read whether the quantitative data support them.

    When to use it

    Use when the constructs are not yet well defined and themes from texts or interviews should shape what you measure.

    When it does not fit

    Fits when constructs are not yet well defined. Misfit: themes cannot be turned into measurable items. The stages depend on each other.

    Output specification

    The result lists the themes drawn from the qualitative material and, for each theme, the measure or hypothesis derived from it. It states what quantitative data would support each hypothesis, and says which themes could not be turned into a measurable item.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureWrites survey items that restate themes and are not measurable, and does not test them against data.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the quantitative stage is missing and when themes come from a small or purposive sample (indirectness).
