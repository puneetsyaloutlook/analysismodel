    TC1-12 Process evaluation and impact evaluation

    Examines how an intervention was delivered and estimates what difference it made, and produces findings on delivery quality or an attributed effect estimate.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: estimate the effect of an action

    What it does

    Process evaluation examines how an intervention was delivered; impact evaluation estimates what difference it made. Artefact: findings on delivery quality, or an attributed effect estimate; read process findings to decide whether a shortfall comes from design or delivery.

    When to use it

    Use when you must separate whether a program was delivered as designed from whether it changed outcomes.

    When it does not fit

    A randomised controlled trial lacks sample size or feasible randomisation, or the control group is affected. Size of impact rarely explains why it occurred.

    Result format

    The result is in two parts: process findings on how well the intervention was delivered, and an attributed effect estimate of what difference it made. It ends by stating whether any shortfall comes from design or from delivery.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureReports an impact estimate from a design with no valid comparison group, or reads a delivery shortfall as a design failure.
    Error sourcesinput data; statistical assumptions; source selection
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the control group is affected, sample size is small, randomisation was not feasible or delivery data are incomplete. Theory-based designs give no precise effect size.

    Related techniques

    • should follow TC1-09 Logic model and theory of change: the logic model names the delivery steps and outcomes to examine
    • pairs with TC1-06 Difference-in-differences Also serves: Compare.: a quasi-experimental estimate supplies the impact half when no trial was run

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Described in government guidance; none gives precise effect sizes from theory-based techniques. (Magenta Book, central government guidance on evaluation; Magenta Book, Annex A, analytical methods for use within an evaluation)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
