    TC1-23 Ladder of inference

    Traces how a conclusion was reached from observed data through selected data and interpretation to belief and produces a stepwise list.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedVerification technique, runs in step 6Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Traces how a conclusion was reached, from observed data through selected data and interpretation to belief. Artefact: a stepwise list; the step where data selection or interpretation was added is the likely error.

    When to use it

    Use when a conclusion seems firmly held and you want to find the step where data were selected or interpreted that may have introduced an error.

    When it does not fit

    A conclusion cannot be linked to observed data.

    Result format

    The result is a stepwise list from the observed data, through the data selected and the interpretation added, to the belief held, with the content of each step written out. It names the step where data selection or interpretation was added as the likely error.

    Method steps

    1. Take one conclusion or belief from the material.
    2. Work down the ladder from it, one rung at a time: the action taken, the belief adopted, the conclusion drawn, the assumptions made, the meanings added, the data selected, and the observable data.
    3. Write what the material says at each rung. Where a rung is not in the material, write that it is not stated.
    4. Mark the rungs that are inference and not observation.
    5. State what observable data would support the conclusion, and what other conclusions the same observable data would allow.
    6. Write the result: the ladder for the conclusion, the unstated rungs, and the alternative conclusions.

    Steps drawn from: Argyris's ladder of inference as described in The Ladder of Inference (University College Dublin copy of the Fifth Discipline Fieldbook text) and the Encyclopedia of Action Research entry.

    Library checks by stakes

    Each stakes level includes the checks of the level below it. Checks that need a person apply at high stakes only, so the process leaves them out at lower stakes (decision 38). Repeatability: judgement dependent.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureReconstructs a plausible ladder after the fact that the author did not follow, and attributes data selection to bias without evidence.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; language model
    Certainty limitsThe ladder is a reconstruction of reasoning and describes it only. Certainty drops when the account of the data is supplied by the person whose reasoning is traced.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with CH-13 Cognitive bias checks and debiasing (including consider-the-opposite): bias checks name the judgement biases at the rungs where selection happened
    • pairs with CH-12 Socratic questioning (Paul and Elder's six question types): Socratic questions elicit the account of the data and reasoning

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Argyris's ladder of inference is a model of the reasoning steps from observable data, through selected data, meanings, assumptions and conclusions, to beliefs and action. It is a reflection tool and was developed within action science. (Fifth Discipline Fieldbook text, UCD copy; Encyclopedia of Action Research) No test of effect on analysis quality was found.

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
