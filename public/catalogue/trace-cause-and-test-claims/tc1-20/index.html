    TC1-20 Argument mapping (argument diagramming)

    Draws claims, reasons and objections as a diagram and produces an argument map that shows isolated claims and reasons not connected to any conclusion.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Draws claims, reasons and objections as a diagram. Artefact: a diagram; read isolated claims and reasons that connect to no conclusion.

    When to use it

    Use when a long text or debate has many claims and you need to see how they connect and which have no support.

    When it does not fit

    The map needs many unstated links to connect its parts.

    Result format

    The result is a diagram of the claims, reasons and objections, with the links between them showing what supports or opposes what and which claim is the main conclusion. It names the isolated claims and the reasons that connect to no conclusion.

    Method steps

    1. State the contention (the main conclusion) as one sentence.
    2. List the reasons given for it. Each reason is a premise or a group of premises that work together.
    3. For each reason, list the evidence or the further reasons that support it.
    4. List the objections to the contention or to a reason, and the rebuttals to each objection.
    5. Put the contention at the top and arrange reasons, objections and rebuttals beneath it, each linked to what it supports or opposes.
    6. Check each link: does the premise, if true, give support to what it is linked to? Mark links that need a hidden premise.
    7. Write the result: the map as an indented list, with the weak links and hidden premises marked.

    Steps drawn from: Federation University, mapping an argument, Deakin University, argument mapping and University of Melbourne, computer-aided argument mapping.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilitymodel alone
    Typical model failureAdds links the author never argued so that fragments appear connected, and leaves out objections.
    Error sourcesinput data; language model; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsThe map describes structure and not truth. Certainty drops when links are inferred and not stated in the text.

    Related techniques

    • pairs with TC1-19 Toulmin argument analysis: Toulmin labelling examines the warrant of a single link that the map shows as a bare arrow
    • pairs with TC1-21 Fallacy analysis (informal fallacies, pragma-dialectical rules): fallacy checks are applied to links the map makes explicit

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. University guides describe argument mapping as a diagram of a contention with its reasons, objections and rebuttals, and the University of Melbourne reports pre- and post-test studies of its use in teaching reasoning, including with intelligence analysts. (Federation University guide; Deakin University guide; University of Melbourne)
