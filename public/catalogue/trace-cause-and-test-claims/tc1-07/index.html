    TC1-07 Quasi-experimental designs (natural experiment, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity)

    Estimates an effect from observational data in which exposure was assigned as if at random (natural experiment, instrument or cutoff) and produces an effect estimate under stated assumptions.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: estimate the effect of an action

    What it does

    Each uses existing data in which exposure is assigned as if at random: by an event or rule (natural experiment), by a variable that changes exposure but affects the outcome only through it (instrument), or by a cutoff (discontinuity). Artefact: an effect estimate under stated assumptions.

    When to use it

    Use when a randomised trial is impossible but an event, rule, instrument or threshold gave some units exposure for reasons unrelated to the outcome.

    When it does not fit

    Assignment is related to outcomes, the instrument is weak or has a direct effect, or units can manipulate their position relative to the cutoff.

    Result format

    The result names the design used (natural experiment, instrument or discontinuity) and describes the event, rule, variable or cutoff that assigns exposure as if at random. It gives an effect estimate and lists the assumptions under which the estimate holds.

    Method steps

    1. State the intervention or policy, the outcome, and the units exposed and not exposed.
    2. Choose the design that fits how exposure was decided: a cut-off score (regression discontinuity), a before and after change in an exposed and an unexposed group (difference-in-differences), a series of measures over time (interrupted time series), or an external source of variation (instrumental variable).
    3. Write the assumptions that design needs. For difference-in-differences, the two groups would have followed parallel trends without the intervention.
    4. Check each assumption against the material and record which hold, which fail and which cannot be checked.
    5. Estimate the effect where the material has the data. Otherwise describe what data the design would need.
    6. Write the result: the design chosen and why, the assumptions with their status, the estimate if any, and the strength of causal statement the design supports.

    Steps drawn from: Kim and Steiner, Quasi-experimental designs for causal inference, Australian Treasury evaluation toolkit and Quasi-experimental study designs series, paper 7.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureAccepts an instrument or cutoff without testing its assumptions and reports a precise estimate that depends on an invalid design.
    Error sourcesinput data; statistical assumptions; data transformation
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the instrument is weak or has a direct effect, when units manipulate the cutoff, or when the estimate applies only to a narrow group of units (risk of bias and indirectness).

    • substitutes for TC1-06 Difference-in-differences Also serves: Compare.: when no untreated comparison group with pre-treatment data exists (time_structure=cross-section) but an instrument or cutoff does

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Quasi-experimental designs estimate causal effects without random assignment. Kim and Steiner outline the strategy, assumptions and estimation for regression discontinuity, instrumental variable, matching and propensity score, and comparative interrupted time series designs. The Australian Treasury toolkit describes regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences and instrumental variables, and notes that they need stronger assumptions than a randomised trial. (Kim and Steiner, PMC; Australian Treasury evaluation toolkit; Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, paper 7)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
