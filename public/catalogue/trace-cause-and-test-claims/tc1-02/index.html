    TC1-02 Fishbone diagram (Ishikawa diagram, cause and effect diagram)

    Groups brainstormed causes of one problem under category branches and produces a fishbone diagram with the problem as its head.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedAnalysis technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: describe

    What it does

    Groups brainstormed causes of one problem under category branches. Artefact: a diagram with the problem as head and categories as ribs; read categories holding many causes and empty categories.

    When to use it

    Use when a team needs to list and sort the many possible causes of one problem before deciding which to investigate.

    When it does not fit

    Many causes fit two categories, a large 'other' rib appears, or problems, causes and symptoms are confused.

    Result format

    The result is a diagram with the problem as the head and category branches as ribs, with the brainstormed causes listed under the category each belongs to. It names the categories holding many causes and the categories left empty.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureFills every category with generic causes that were not raised by anyone and treats the count of causes on a branch as evidence of importance.
    Error sourcesinput data; coder or rater judgement; expert opinion
    Certainty limitsCertainty is low because the diagram records proposed causes and not tested ones. It falls further when all causes come from one brainstorming group.

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. One of the seven basic tools of quality control; application claims on the page have 'citation needed' tags; the BMJ commentary lists it among alternatives to five whys without effect evidence. (Wikipedia, Ishikawa diagram; BMJ Quality and Safety 2017;26:671, on the five whys)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
