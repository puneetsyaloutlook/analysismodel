    TC-04 Claim and citation verification against source text (fact verification; claim checking; citation support check; quotation accuracy check)

    Labels each claim as supported, refuted or not enough information against retrieved evidence, or rates whether a cited source supports its claim, and produces a label with evidence sentences.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: TestedPhase: validateClaim ceiling: describe

    What it does

    Labels a claim as supported, refuted or not enough information against retrieved evidence, or checks that a cited source supports the claim it is attached to. Artefact: a label with evidence sentences, or a supported, minor-error or major-error rating per citation. Read the evidence sentences, and read repeated claims for a shared origin.

    When to use it

    Use when you need to check whether claims in a text, or the sources cited for them, are actually backed by the source text.

    When it does not fit

    Fits when the corpus or cited source contains the relevant sentence. Misfit: evidence spread across documents or needing inference, paywalled sources, and opinions or predictions extracted as claims. The minor-error class is subjective (kappa of 0.26 in one study).

    Output specification

    The result is a table with one row per claim or citation, giving the claim, the source, the label (supported, refuted or not enough information, or a supported, minor-error or major-error rating) and the evidence sentences behind it. It names the repeated claims that share an origin.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureLabels a claim as supported because a similar sentence exists, and counts a claim repeated across sources as independently confirmed.
    Error sourcesinput data; language model; source selection; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when evidence is spread across documents, needs inference or is behind a paywall, when labels are judged subjectively, and when repeated claims share one origin (inconsistency and indirectness).

    Related techniques

    • should follow CK-19 Atomic claim decomposition and scoring (FActScore; atomic fact checking): atomic claim decomposition splits the text into the claims to be checked
    • pairs with CK-17 Source-span and quotation check by code (quote-and-verify; extractive grounding; citation verification): a string match by code confirms that each cited passage exists
    • pairs with IS-06 Source dependence and copy detection (source independence analysis): repeated claims may share one origin and so do not corroborate each other

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. A 2018 baseline pipeline reached 31.87 per cent accuracy even with correct evidence supplied, and annotators agreed at Fleiss kappa 0.6841 (not a statement about current models). A meta-analysis of 28 medical studies gave a pooled citation error rate of 25.4 per cent, with 11.9 per cent major errors. (Thorne et al., FEVER, arXiv 1803.05355; Jergas and Baethge, PeerJ 2015)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
