    TC1-15 Bayesian updating (likelihood reasoning, likelihood ratio, Bayes factor)

    Revises the probability of a hypothesis in proportion to how much more likely the evidence is under it than under alternatives and produces a posterior probability or odds.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: UnconfirmedAnalysis technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: predict, without claiming a cause

    What it does

    Revises the probability of a hypothesis in proportion to how much more likely the evidence is under it than under the alternatives. Artefact: a posterior probability or odds; read the likelihood ratio as the strength of the evidence.

    When to use it

    Use when you hold a prior view on a hypothesis and need to say how far a piece of evidence should move it.

    When it does not fit

    The prior or the likelihoods are guesses presented as precise numbers.

    Result format

    The result gives a prior probability for the hypothesis, a likelihood of each evidence item under the hypothesis and under the alternatives, and the likelihood ratio for each item. It ends with a posterior probability or odds, with the likelihood ratio read as the strength of the evidence.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureStates posterior probabilities to false precision from invented priors and likelihoods, or multiplies likelihoods of dependent evidence.
    Error sourcesinput data; expert opinion; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty is limited by the quality of the prior and the likelihoods, which are usually judgements. It drops further when evidence items are dependent or when the hypothesis set is incomplete (imprecision).

    Related techniques

    • substitutes for TC1-13 Analysis of competing hypotheses (ACH): when evidence items can be given likelihoods as numbers (variable_types=ratio|counts or fractions)
