    TC1-15 Bayesian updating (likelihood reasoning, likelihood ratio, Bayes factor)

    Revises the probability of a hypothesis in proportion to how much more likely the evidence is under it than under alternatives and produces a posterior probability or odds.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: DocumentedPrimary technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: predict, without claiming a cause

    What it does

    Revises the probability of a hypothesis in proportion to how much more likely the evidence is under it than under the alternatives. Artefact: a posterior probability or odds; read the likelihood ratio as the strength of the evidence.

    When to use it

    Use when you hold a prior view on a hypothesis and need to say how far a piece of evidence should move it.

    When it does not fit

    The prior or the likelihoods are guesses presented as precise numbers.

    Result format

    The result gives a prior probability for the hypothesis, a likelihood of each evidence item under the hypothesis and under the alternatives, and the likelihood ratio for each item. It ends with a posterior probability or odds, with the likelihood ratio read as the strength of the evidence.

    Method steps

    1. List the hypotheses that could explain the observation. They must be exclusive and together cover the possibilities.
    2. Write a prior probability for each hypothesis, before the evidence, and the basis for it.
    3. For each piece of evidence, write the likelihood: the probability of seeing that evidence if each hypothesis were true.
    4. Multiply prior by likelihood for each hypothesis (the unnormalised posterior).
    5. Divide each product by the sum of the products, so the posteriors add to one.
    6. For further evidence, use the posterior as the new prior and repeat steps 3 to 5.
    7. Write the result: the table of priors, likelihoods and posteriors, and how much the answer changes if the priors change.

    Steps drawn from: MIT OpenCourseWare 18.05, Bayesian updating with discrete priors and Manning, Grokking Bayes, chapter 2.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureStates posterior probabilities to false precision from invented priors and likelihoods, or multiplies likelihoods of dependent evidence.
    Error sourcesinput data; expert opinion; statistical assumptions
    Certainty limitsCertainty is limited by the quality of the prior and the likelihoods, which are usually judgements. It drops further when evidence items are dependent or when the hypothesis set is incomplete (imprecision).

    Evidence and tag basis

    Documented. Bayesian updating uses data to revise the probability of each hypothesis: the Bayes numerator (prior times likelihood) is rescaled to sum to one to give the posterior. (MIT 18.05, Bayesian updating with discrete priors; Manning, Grokking Bayes, chapter 2)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
