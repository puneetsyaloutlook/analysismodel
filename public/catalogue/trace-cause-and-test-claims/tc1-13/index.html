    TC1-13 Analysis of competing hypotheses (ACH)

    Lists plausible hypotheses, rates each evidence item against each, and produces a hypothesis by evidence matrix aimed at disproving hypotheses.

    Trace cause and test claimsEvidence: TestedAnalysis technique, runs in step 5.3Strongest statement: show a link between things

    What it does

    Lists all plausible hypotheses and rates each evidence item against each, aiming to disprove hypotheses. Artefact: a hypothesis by evidence matrix; read the evidence that discriminates between hypotheses and the hypothesis with the fewest inconsistencies.

    When to use it

    Use when several explanations are plausible and you need to see which evidence actually discriminates between them.

    When it does not fit

    The hypothesis list is incomplete, analysts skip steps, or non-diagnostic evidence shifts the result.

    Result format

    The result lists the hypotheses and gives a matrix of evidence items against hypotheses with a consistency rating in each cell. It names the evidence items that discriminate between hypotheses and the hypothesis with the fewest inconsistencies.

    Failure modes and limits

    Model suitabilityhuman judgement required
    Typical model failureRates most evidence items as consistent with every hypothesis so that nothing discriminates, and omits the hypothesis the analyst finds unlikely.
    Error sourcesinput data; source selection; coder or rater judgement
    Certainty limitsCertainty drops when the hypothesis list is incomplete, when evidence is not diagnostic, or when all ratings come from one analyst (risk of bias and inconsistency).

    Related techniques

    • pairs with TC1-16 Disconfirmation search (falsification, seeking disconfirming evidence): a disconfirmation search tests the hypothesis that survives the matrix
    • substitutes for TC1-15 Bayesian updating (likelihood reasoning, likelihood ratio, Bayes factor): when evidence is available only as narrative and probabilities cannot be stated (access_depth=narrative only)
    • pairs with CH-01 Key assumptions check (assumptions check): a key assumptions check exposes premises shared by every hypothesis

    Evidence and tag basis

    Tested. Tested in experiments with no accuracy benefit and some signs of harm to coherence; see the evidence section. Heuer concedes it will not always yield the right answer. (Dhami, Belton and Mandel (2019), Strathprints record; Karvetski and Mandel (2020), Cambridge Core, does ACH help coherence; Cambridge Core paper on what works and what fails in improving analysts' accuracy; Heuer, Psychology of Intelligence Analysis)

    Tag basis: input profile inference; selection inference; sequencing inference; robustness inference. All tag values are inference unless a source is named.
