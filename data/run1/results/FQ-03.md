# Definitional analysis of H1 to H3

Each hypothesis depends on terms that can be read more than one way. Findings apply to this sample of eight only.

## Glossary

**Satisfying (H1).** A participant evaluates the AI scenario positively, in their own words, as an experience.
- Borderline: Q2 and Q3 praise human-likeness ("like talking to a real person", "seamless") rather than satisfaction directly. Q5 turns positive only after initial reluctance.

**Traditional voice automation (H1).** Earlier phone systems that respond to menus or keywords.
- Borderline: Q1 compares the AI with chatbots, which this definition excludes. Q2 and Q3 name no system.

**Hang up immediately (H2).** The call ends during the introduction, before any task content.
- Borderline: Q8 ("I would've definitely have hung up at the intro") fits the timing, but it is hypothetical and its stated reason is length, not discovering an AI. Q10's hang-up (Q10) comes only after a human is refused, so it is not immediate.

**Learning it is an AI (H2).** The participant recognises or is told the agent is not human.
- Borderline: Q4 says it "sounds very much like an AI voice", which is recognition by sound. The material does not say whether disclosure occurred.

**Immediately ask for a human (H3).** A request for a human during the introduction, before engaging with the task.
- Borderline: Q9 names the introduction but is hedged ("probably"). Q10's request has no stated timing. Q5 says "I'd be probably wanting to speak to someone", which is a wish, not a request. Q6 mentions the option to ask for a human at the beginning, but P1 does not say they would use it.

## Where answers differ by definition

- H1: Under a strict reading, only Q1 compares with earlier automation, and it is about chatbots. Under a loose reading, Q1 to Q3 support H1 and Q4, Q5 and Q7 complicate it.
- H2: Q8 contradicts H2 only if hypothetical statements count as behaviour.
- H3: Q9 contradicts H3 only if "probably" counts as a request.

## Assumptions and limitations

The technique assumed ten quotes suffice to test each definition. It could not count how many of the eight participants fall into each case, because only some are quoted. Every behavioural quote is hypothetical, since participants heard prerecorded scenarios and no one actually hung up or asked.

## Alternative explanations

The positive remarks in Q2 and Q3 may reflect surprise at how human the voice sounded, not satisfaction. The hang-up and human requests in Q8 to Q10 may reflect the length of the introduction or the stressful context of a car accident, not the AI itself.
