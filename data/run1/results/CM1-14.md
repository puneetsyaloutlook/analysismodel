# Absence analysis: what the quotes leave out

## Reference structure used

The reference structure is the one the material itself states: four prerecorded scenarios (motor claim lodgement, policy sales, premium disputes, policy cancellation), eight participants (P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P8, P9, P10) and the three hypotheses H1 to H3. Nothing outside the material was gathered.

## Expected but absent items

| Item | What was expected, and why | Strength of expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Any quote from P8 or P9 | Each of the eight listened to all four scenarios, so each would be expected to have a reaction. However, the material says "Only some participants are quoted", so selection by the compiler explains the gap without any need for a finding about P8 or P9. | Weak |
| Quotes tied to policy sales, premium disputes or policy cancellation | Four scenarios were played, so reactions to each would be expected. Only Q7 names a scenario (a car accident, which matches motor claim lodgement), and it is a hypothetical. Ten quotes are too few to cover four scenarios across eight people. | Weak to moderate |
| A direct comparison with traditional voice automation (H1) | H1 needs a contrast with voice automation. Q1 contrasts the AI with "chat bots", which are text tools. Q2 contrasts it with a "robot voice", and Q3 with having to "repeat yourself", but neither names voice automation. No quote rates satisfaction. | Moderate |
| An observed hang-up or request for a human (H2, H3) | The scenarios were prerecorded, so no participant could hang up or ask for anyone. Q8, Q9 and Q10 are all conditional ("would've", "I would do it"). Observed behaviour was never possible, so its absence says nothing. | Not applicable |
| Disclosure of the AI in the scenarios | H2 and H3 turn on the moment a customer learns it is an AI. Only Q4 ("sounds very much like an AI voice") and Q6 (mention of a human option at the beginning) touch this. The material does not say how or when the scenarios disclosed it. | Moderate |
| Positive quotes from P10, P5 and P4 about the AI | If H1 held broadly, some positive remark from these participants would be expected. P4 gives one (Q5), though it is qualified. P5 and P10 give none in the quoted set, but their quotes may simply be the ones chosen. | Weak |

## Conclusion

Most of these absences are explained by how the material was assembled: a partial set of quotes and prerecorded scenarios. They are limited evidence at best. The strongest expectation is the missing comparison with voice automation, which means the quotes cannot confirm H1 as worded. Q2 and Q3 support only a weaker claim, that P2 and P3 found the AI natural. For H2 and H3, the quotes show intentions, with P10 (Q8) and P5 (Q10) saying they would hang up and P5 (Q10) and P4 (Q9) saying they would ask for a human. These cut against both hypotheses for those three participants.

## Assumptions and limitations

The technique assumes the quoted set is a fair sample of what was said. It is not: the compiler chose it and the selection rule is unknown. The technique cannot show what P8 and P9 thought, and cannot show what the full transcripts contain.

## Alternative explanations

The thin coverage of three scenarios may reflect ordinary variation in what participants chose to comment on, not a lack of reaction. The missing voice automation comparison may mean the interview guide never asked for one, so the gap would belong to the design and not to the participants.
