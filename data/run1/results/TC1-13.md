# Analysis of competing hypotheses: voice AI interviews

## Hypotheses and rivals

- H1: voice AI is more satisfying than traditional voice automation. Rival H1r: it is no more satisfying, or less.
- H2: customers do not hang up immediately on learning it is an AI. Rival H2r: they hang up at or near the start.
- H3: customers do not immediately ask for a human. Rival H3r: they ask for a human at the start.

## Matrix

C = consistent, I = inconsistent, N = not diagnostic. Quotes are classed by their content, and most are hypothetical.

## What discriminates

For H1, Q2 and Q3 favour H1 over H1r, while Q4, Q7 and Q8 favour H1r. Q1 compares the AI with website chatbots, not voice automation, so it is only weak support. Q6 does not discriminate, because the annoyance could refer to earlier AI experiences.

For H2, only Q8 (P10: "I would've definitely have hung up at the intro") and Q5 (P4 stays on) separate H2 from H2r. Q10 mentions hanging up only after a human is refused.

For H3, Q5, Q9 and Q10 each count against H3 and for H3r. Q9 says asking for a human at the introduction is "probably where I would do it".

## Fewest inconsistencies

H1 has three inconsistencies and H1r has four, so the evidence is split. H2 and H2r each have one, so the matrix does not separate them. H3r has none, and H3 has three. H3, as worded, is the weakest hypothesis. These three items come from only two participants, P4 and P5.

## Assumptions and limitations

The technique assumed that each quote reflects a stable view and that quotes are comparable evidence. Every ranking here rests on single statements. Most are conditional ("would have"), so none records an actual hang-up or request. The material gives no counts and does not say how the ten quotes were chosen. P8 and P9 are not quoted, so the matrix says nothing about them. Q1 compares the AI with chatbots, not voice automation, which limits its bearing on H1. The matrix cannot weight evidence by strength.

## Alternative explanations

The H3 result may reflect scenario type, not attitude to AI. P5 spoke about a car accident, where distress may drive a request for a human. P10's complaint in Q8 is about the length of the introduction and the American voice, so it may not be about AI disclosure at all. The split on H1 may reflect individual differences in prior experience with chatbots, as P1 gives both a positive (Q1) and a negative (Q6) reaction.
