# Hypothesis tree: do the interviews support H1 to H3?

## Tree

**Main claim: the interviews support H1, H2 and H3.** True only if all three branches are true.

**H1: voice AI is more satisfying than traditional voice automation**
- 1a. Participants react positively. Q1 (P1), Q2 (P2) and Q3 (P3) are positive. Q4 (P10), Q6 (P1) and Q7 (P5) are negative. Mixed.
- 1b. Participants rate it above traditional voice automation. Q1 compares with website chatbots, not voice automation. Q2 contrasts it with "a robot voice", a loose proxy. Thin.

**H2: customers do not hang up immediately on learning it is an AI**
- 2a. Participants who learn it is an AI stay on the call. Q8 (P10) says "I would've definitely have hung up at the intro." Q10 (P5) says hanging up is likely if no human is offered. Refuted for these two.
- 2b. Other participants stay on the call. No quote says so directly. Not established.

**H3: customers do not immediately ask for a human agent**
- 3a. Participants proceed without asking for a human. Q9 (P4) and Q10 (P5) say they would ask. Q5 (P4) says "I'd be probably wanting to speak to someone" at first. Refuted for these two.
- 3b. Any initial wish for a human fades. Q5 (P4) supports this for one participant only.

## Leaf claims for which no evidence is possible

- 1b in full: no participant heard a traditional voice automation scenario, so no direct comparison exists.
- Actual hang-ups (2a, 2b) and actual requests for a human (3a): the scenarios were prerecorded, so every behaviour quote is a stated intention.
- Any claim about P8 and P9: no quote is given for them.
- Satisfaction on a common measure: no ratings appear in the material.

## Reading

H2 and H3 each have a refuting branch in this sample (P10 for H2; P4 and P5 for H3), so neither holds as stated. H1 has support on 1a only and cannot be tested on 1b.

## Assumptions and limitations

The tree assumes the hypotheses mean what they say, including "immediately", and that stated intentions are a fair guide to behaviour. The material is ten quotes, and how they were selected is unknown. The tree cannot show how common any view is.

## Alternative explanations

The refutations may reflect scenario design, not voice AI itself. Q7 (P5) ties frustration to a car accident, while Q4 (P10) and Q8 (P10) point to an American voice and a long introduction. The praise in Q2 and Q3 may reflect the quality of these particular recordings.
