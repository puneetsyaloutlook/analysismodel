# Comparative case study: eight interviews against H1 to H3

Each participant is a case asked the same three questions. H1: is voice AI more satisfying than earlier automation? H2: would they hang up on learning it is an AI? H3: would they ask for a human at once? Cells use only the quotes. P8 and P9 have no quotes.

| Case | H1 | H2 | H3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Mixed: Q1 "pretty dynamic"; Q6 annoyed | No evidence | Unclear (Q6) |
| P2 | Supportive (Q2), no comparison | No evidence | No evidence |
| P3 | Supportive (Q3) | No evidence | No evidence |
| P4 | Supportive after a delay (Q5) | No evidence | Would, at first (Q5, Q9) |
| P5 | Against, accident scenario (Q7) | Conditional hang-up (Q10) | Would ask (Q10) |
| P10 | Against, voice and accent (Q4) | Would hang up at the intro (Q8) | No evidence |
| P8, P9 | No quotes | No quotes | No quotes |

## Consistent patterns

- H1: P1 to P4 react positively, but only P1 compares with earlier systems, and that comparison is with chatbots, not voice automation. The praise describes the AI, not a ranking.
- Negative reactions (P5, P10, P1 in Q6) concern the voice, the accent, the length of the introduction and a stressful scenario.

## Deviant cases

- P10 and P5 say they would hang up (Q8, Q10), against H2.
- P4 and P5 say they would ask for a human (Q5, Q9, Q10), against H3. P4's wish fades as the call continues.

Only these participants speak to H2 or H3, and all point against the hypotheses. Four cases are silent on them. H1 has the most support, but it rests on four cases and a weak comparison.

## Assumptions and limitations

The technique assumes each case was chosen for a stated reason. The material says only that some participants are quoted, so selection could favour either side. Most cells are empty because each quote addressed one question at most. Every statement reacts to prerecorded scenarios, and several are hypothetical ("would have"). Nothing here extends beyond this sample.

## Alternative explanations

The negative H2 and H3 cases may reflect the scenario and not the technology: P5 refers to a car accident and P10 to a long introduction. The positive H1 reactions may reflect a good recording, not voice AI in general. Vivid quotes may also have been chosen, overstating both sides.
