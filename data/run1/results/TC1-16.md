# Disconfirmation search on the favoured reading

## Claim under test

The favoured reading across the seven findings is this. In the eight interviews, H1 cannot be tested as worded and has support only for the AI sounding natural. H2 and H3 are contradicted for P10, P5 and P4, but only by hypothetical statements. P8 and P9 contribute nothing.

## Evidence that would refute it (written before searching)

1. A direct comparison with traditional voice automation that favours the AI.
2. A participant who stays on the call after learning it is an AI.
3. Evidence that the hang-up in Q8 or Q10 was immediate and caused by learning it is an AI.
4. A participant who proceeds without asking for a human.
5. Any reaction from P8 or P9.
6. Observed, not hypothetical, behaviour.

## Search and results

The search was one full pass over Q1 to Q10, reading each quote for the item in question. It was a single pass over a small set, so it is shallow.

1. **Partly found.** Q1 compares the AI with website chatbots, and Q2 contrasts it with "a robot voice". Neither names voice automation, so the claim that H1 is untested survives.
2. **Found for one participant.** Q5 (P4): "as the process goes on, you realise it's actually, um, a really good process." This shows a wish for a human fading, so H2 is not contradicted for P4.
3. **Not found, and the reverse was found.** Q8 gives the reason as "it was way too long", and Q10 hangs up only after a human is refused. Neither is an immediate hang-up on learning it is an AI. This weakens "contradicted" for H2.
4. **Not found as a stated behaviour.** Q6 mentions the human option at the beginning but P1 does not say they would use it. Q2 and Q3 are silent on asking.
5. **Not found.** No quote from P8 or P9 exists, so this search could not be run.
6. **Not found.** The scenarios were prerecorded.

## What this means for the claim

The claim survives in a weaker form. H1 is still untested. H2 is the weakest part of the favoured reading: Q8 is hypothetical and gives length as its reason, Q10 is conditional, and Q5 shows one participant staying on. H2 is better described as not supported by the quotes than as contradicted. H3 is more robust. Q9 and Q10 both point to asking for a human, though Q9 is hedged ("probably") and Q10 gives no timing.

Not finding refuting evidence for items 4 to 6 does not confirm the claim. It reflects the limits of the material.

## Assumptions and limitations

The search used only the ten quotes and the seven findings. It cannot see full transcripts or the rule used to select quotes. The refuting items were written after the findings were read, so they may reflect those findings.

## Alternative explanations

Q8 and Q10 may reflect the length of the introduction and the car accident context, not the AI. Q5 may reflect how P4 responded to this recording, not a general pattern.
