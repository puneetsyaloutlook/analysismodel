## Reading across the rows

P2 and P3 are positive on every theme where they have data, and they say nothing on H2 or H3. P4 and P5 are the main sources for H3, and both describe a possible request for a human, so H3 has support against it in this sample. P5 and P10 give the only hang up material (Q10, Q8). P1 is mixed: Q1 is positive and Q6 is negative. P8 and P9 have no quoted data, so they cannot be read either way.

## Reading down the columns

H1: Q1, Q2 and Q3 lean positive, Q4, Q6 and Q7 lean negative, and Q5 moves from negative to positive. Only Q1 and Q2 compare against earlier systems, and Q1 compares with chatbots, not voice automation. H1 is therefore not tested directly.

H2: Q8 and Q10 are both hypothetical and both conditional. Q8 ties the hang up to the length of the introduction, not to learning it was an AI. H2 is weakly contradicted for two participants and silent for six.

H3: Q9 and Q10 run against H3, with Q5 as a partial qualifier. Q6 is ambiguous.

## Empty cells

P8 and P9 are empty throughout. P2 and P3 are empty on H2 and H3, and P1, P4 and P10 are each empty on one theme. The cell pattern shows where quotes exist. It is not a response rate.

## Assumptions and limitations

The matrix assumes the ten quotes are a fair record of each participant, but the material says only some participants are quoted. Empty cells therefore mean "not quoted", not "no view". Coding used the quote text alone, with no transcripts or scenario detail. The matrix cannot show which of the four scenarios each remark concerns, and cannot give frequencies.

## Alternative explanations

The negative H2 and H3 cells may reflect the scenarios, such as the motor claim in Q7, or the specific voice and long introduction (Q4, Q7, Q8), not AI voice support in general. The positive H1 cells may reflect participants judging this one recording against general expectations, not against traditional voice automation. Quote selection may also favour vivid reactions.
