A technique is a named way of analysing something, such as five whys, difference-in-differences or lateral reading. The catalogue holds 236 of them. Each has a plain description of what it does and what it produces, a statement of when it fits and when it does not, the conditions your material must meet, and a result format that tells the model what a run of the technique must contain.

The catalogue is not a textbook. It records enough about each technique for the process to do three things without a person: decide whether the technique can run on your material (step 2), choose the ones that fit your request (step 3), and put them in a workable order with the right checks (steps 4 to 6). The [process pages](/process/) explain those steps.

## How the techniques are grouped

The catalogue has 12 families. A family groups techniques by what they do for a request, such as framing a question, comparing cases, or checking results. The family does not decide when a technique runs. That depends on the kind of technique (question, preparation, primary, verification or challenge), which the [dictionary](/reference/phases-and-tiers/) defines.

## How to read a technique page

Each technique page has these parts, in this order.

- **The header** gives the identifier, the family, the evidence status, the kind of technique and the step it runs in, and the strongest statement the technique can support. Evidence status says how well the technique itself is supported: tested (a published study measured its effect or accuracy), documented (described in a text or guidance, without a test), practice (used with no published description found) or unconfirmed (no source could be opened). Across the catalogue, 33 are tested, 150 documented, 13 practice and 40 unconfirmed.
- **What it does, when to use it and when it does not fit** describe the technique in the words a user would use.
- **Result format** says what a run must produce.
- **Requirements on the material** list what the material must be like, such as record-level data or more than one source. Step 2 uses these to remove techniques that cannot run.
- **Choosing and ordering** and **library checks by stakes** show how the process places the technique and which checks it needs at each stakes level.
- **Failure modes and limits, related techniques, and evidence and tag basis** say where the technique goes wrong, which techniques it pairs with or substitutes for, and how the tags were decided. Most tags are inference unless the page names a source.

The property names in the requirements table link to their definitions in the [dictionary](/reference/).
