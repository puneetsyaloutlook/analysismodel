# Internal consistency check of the findings on the adoption article

## Scope

The brief asks whether the article accurately diagnoses its phenomenon and whether its point of view is valid. This check examines the seven result files for steps 5.1 to 5.3 (FQ-03, TC1-19, TC1-21, CM1-01, CM1-14, DM-07, TC1-13), not the article. It asks whether their claims, figures and terms are compatible with each other and with the article text they quote. The favoured reading is their joint conclusion: the narrow diagnosis (a usage count cannot show designed use versus workaround, or work that no longer exists) follows from how usage is counted, while the premises about prevalence, the wider claims and the proposed remedy are asserted more than shown. Consistency does not show that this reading is true. It shows only that the files do not contradict each other on it, except where listed below.

Method steps were followed in order. Step 1 listed the claims. Step 2 compared them by cluster, because the seven files make roughly 100 separate statements, and every pair within a cluster and across the clusters that share a topic was marked. Step 3 recomputed the numbers. Step 4 checked terms. Step 5 is the list of findings.

## Step 5: findings

### 1. "Reasoning is sound" against the tensions the same files report

- Claim A: TC1-13 says "the article's diagnosis reads as plausible and its reasoning as internally sound". CM1-14 says "The honest reading is that the reasoning is coherent but the evidence for its premises is thin and not shown."
- Claim B: FQ-03, TC1-19 and TC1-21 each report inconsistencies inside the article's reasoning: the heading "Each workaround shows where the design failed" against the Team and Leadership examples; "no earlier version of the work to compare it with" against the medium-term tier; "few organisations can trace either back to AI specifically" (TC1-21 entry 11) against "the gain can be measured"; and a definition of adoption that makes the claim true by construction.
- Status: in conflict as worded. Both cannot stand unqualified. A consistent statement is that the narrow logic of the usage-count argument is sound while the wider reasoning has internal tensions. The favoured reading should be stated in that scoped form. Each file does offer a definitional reconciliation in its alternative explanations, so the conflict may be one of emphasis.

### 2. Whether the Team and Leadership examples belong to the term "workaround"

- Claim A: FQ-03 marks the team, leadership and task examples as borderline and says that, if a workaround must route work around a tool, the team and leadership examples fall outside the term.
- Claim B: TC1-19 (Argument 2) calls the same two examples "cases of gaming the metric, which is a different problem from a design workaround", and DM-07 sorts them into service quality and "outside the lens" while sorting the other three workaround levels and the claim "each workaround shows which part of the design failed" (C16) into system quality.
- Status: the files agree on the substance. The inconsistency is inside DM-07: C16 is a claim about every workaround but is placed in one category, system quality, while two of the five workaround levels are placed elsewhere. DM-07 acknowledges the cause (L26 and L27) in prose but does not change the table. The table therefore understates the span of C16.

### 3. Scope of the before-and-after comparison

- Claim A: DM-07 says "The medium and longer tiers (L44, L45) depend on before and after comparison".
- Claim B: TC1-19 and CM1-14 attribute the comparison only to the medium-term tier, and the article agrees. The longer-term tier (L45) asks whether each new job "produces the results it was created for", which is not a comparison with an earlier state.
- Status: DM-07 conflicts with the article text and with TC1-19 and CM1-14. The tension with removed work applies to the medium tier alone, so DM-07 overstates it.

### 4. Strength and defect status of the same argument

- Claim A: TC1-19 (K3) calls Argument 2 "the strongest argument in the article, because G1 and G2 follow from how usage counting works".
- Claim B: TC1-21 (entry 8) flags the warrant for that argument ("A workaround is its opposite", with adoption defined as designed use) as begging the question, and FQ-03 says the central claim is "partly true by definition".
- Status: apparent conflict only. The factual part (a licence holder who opens the tool still counts as active) is sound, as TC1-21 itself says in its "not flagged" list. The claim that counts "miss workarounds" depends on the definition. The two assessments apply to different parts of the argument. The joint reading should say which part is strong and which is definitional, because "strongest" and "circular" are not reconcilable if left unscoped.

### 5. Whether the claim that nobody adopts AI is needed by the diagnosis

- Claim A: TC1-19 calls Argument 4 "the weakest link in the chain" and lists it with the weaker moves from diagnosis to point of view.
- Claim B: TC1-21 (entry 5) says "The article's own point stands without this step, because the two-case typology does not need the word to be literally wrong."
- Status: partly in conflict. If the argument is a chain, a weak link affects the conclusion. If it is separable, it does not. The files disagree on which, and TC1-19 says elsewhere (alternative explanations and overall reading) that the diagnosis is supported at the level of logic regardless. The favoured reading should treat Argument 4 as separable from the diagnosis and relevant to the point of view only.

### 6. Whether the tension on removed work is real or one of scale

- Claim A: TC1-19 and CM1-14 treat the conflict between "no earlier version" and the medium-term comparison as a defect of the article.
- Claim B: FQ-03 says a baseline does exist for the document example (the earlier volume of requests), and offers in its alternative explanations that the two statements concern different scales. TC1-19 offers sequencing.
- Status: not a contradiction between files, but the files give three different resolutions (defect, difference of scale, sequencing) and the joint conclusion should not present any one as settled. FQ-03's point that a baseline may exist also bears on whether "An adoption measure has no way to record this value" can be read as "no measure can".

### 7. An omission in TC1-21

- TC1-21 lists the removed-work claim as coherent "apart from the points in entry 12" and does not mention the medium-term tension that four other files report. This is not a contradiction. It means the favoured reading cannot cite TC1-21 as agreeing on K6.

### 8. A mismatch between a finding and the article's example levels

- Claim A: CM1-01 says the 78% bring-your-own-tools figure "is consistent with the interface and task workaround of using a personal tool".
- Claim B: the article places the personal-tool example at the Interface level only ("Or the approved tool is slow, so people use a personal one instead."). The Task example is about staff altering records. TC1-13 (E2a) correctly says "interface example".
- Status: CM1-01 is inconsistent with the article and with TC1-13 on this item. The effect is small.

### 9. Locator and placement slips

- DM-07 cites the "none of the tiers is new" paragraph as L47. In the article file L47 is blank and the paragraph is L48 (also item C18). The content reference is clear, so the error is only in the number.
- TC1-19 (Argument 4) calls "AI is not a single thing that a person chooses and takes responsibility for" the "article's opening sentence". It is the first sentence under the heading "People use tools and learn new jobs", not the opening of the article.
- FQ-03 cites a section "Adoption" for several adoption quotations. The article has no heading of that name (the passage is the second paragraph, L5), and FQ-03 says passages are identified by section heading.
- TC1-19 says in its first paragraph "I identified five arguments", then presents six (Arguments 1 to 6), and its limitations say "I split it into six arguments by topic". The count in the first paragraph is wrong.

None of these changes a conclusion, but the first and the last would stop a reader reconciling the file against the article or against itself.

### 10. Terms used in different senses across the files

- "Evidence". FQ-03 defines it as a checkable source or an argument with stated premises. TC1-13 and DM-07 use "evidence" for items in a matrix or table. CM1-01 and TC1-13 treat E1 and E2 as evidence about the present. All four files agree that the article names no checkable source, so the difference does not affect the conclusion.
- "Adoption". FQ-03 keeps two senses (measured and effective). DM-07 treats adoption as the use dimension only. CM1-14 maps it onto "use". TC1-13 H4 refers to organisations measuring "outcomes as well". These are compatible, but only FQ-03 states that the article moves between senses, and the others read it in the narrow counted sense. The favoured reading that "adoption measures miss value" holds only in the measured sense.
- "Workaround". Used for five levels by all files, and for the article's closing line "adoption is itself a workaround" by FQ-03 and TC1-21 (as a stretch) and DM-07 (as outside the lens). The files agree this third use is different.
- "Design". TC1-19 and TC1-21 note that the article's heading uses "design" in a sense that must include workload and incentives for the heading to hold. DM-07 and FQ-03 use "design" for system quality. This is a difference of definition and not a contradiction.

### 11. Pairs checked where no conflict was found

- The five-way agreement that prevalence is unsourced (K7) holds in all seven files with the same quotation.
- The same article quotations are used consistently wherever they recur (for example "These are actual observations from research on tools and agentic automation", "none of these tiers is new", "we've done this before with methods that still work").
- The figures for E1 and E2 do not differ between CM1-01 and TC1-13.
- The mapping of article topics to model dimensions in DM-07 and CM1-14 does not differ.
- The ordering H2 ahead of H3 and H4 in TC1-13 follows its own counts and is described there as not firm, which agrees with its limitations.

## What the check shows

The files agree closely on the facts: the quotations, the figures and the counts reconcile, apart from one wrong line number, one wrong count in a first paragraph and one misplaced level in CM1-01. The substantive conflicts concern how strongly the joint conclusion can be stated. "Reasoning sound" (finding 1), "strongest argument" (finding 4) and "weakest link" (finding 5) are each stronger than the other files allow unless scoped to the narrow diagnosis. DM-07 overstates the comparison problem (finding 3) and under-specifies the heading claim in its table (finding 2). The favoured reading survives if it is worded as: the logic of the usage-count argument is sound; the definitional step, the heading about design failure, the removed-work claim against the medium tier, and the remedy are weaker; prevalence is unsupported.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The comparison was done by cluster, because the seven files contain too many statements to compare one by one. A contradiction between two minor statements in different clusters could be missed.
- The check found internal conflicts among the files and against the article text. It does not test whether the E1, E2, E3 or E4 summaries quoted in the files are accurate, because those sources are outside the permitted material, so a figure repeated identically in two files could still be wrong.
- Several apparent conflicts are differences of definition (findings 4, 5 and 10). Reporting them risks the failure this technique warns about, so each is marked as apparent or scoped.
- Arithmetic checks covered the TC1-13 matrix, the DM-07 table and the CM1-14 term counts. Counts in DM-07 and TC1-13 were produced by the files' authors by code and were recounted here by hand from the printed tables. The CM1-14 counts were recounted by text search of the article.
- The files quote the article as supplied, and the supplied text may differ from the published version.
- Consistency is not correctness. All seven files could share a mistaken premise, for example about what counts as a workaround, without any conflict showing here.

## Alternative explanations

- The conflicts in findings 1, 4 and 5 may come from each technique having a different purpose. A glossary, a Toulmin diagram and a fallacy list judge the same sentence by different tests, so they will rate its strength differently without any file being wrong.
- The slips in finding 9 and the CM1-01 mismatch may be drafting errors that arose because the article has no item IDs, so each file had to invent its own locators. This would explain why the errors are about location and not about substance.
- The close agreement among the files may be partly produced by a shared reading of the article, because they were run on one text with the same brief. If so, agreement is weaker confirmation than it appears.
- Finding 3 may reflect DM-07 using "longer tier" loosely for "the later tiers", which would make its sentence about the medium tier correct and its mention of L45 a slip.
