# Disconfirmation search on the favoured reading of the voice AI interviews

## Claim under test

The eight result files agree closely enough to give one favoured reading. It is stated here precisely, as patterns within this sample only (six of eight participants are quoted: P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P10).

- C1. H1 is partly supported. P1, P2 and P3 speak well of the AI, but no quote compares it with traditional voice automation, so the comparison in H1 cannot be tested.
- C2. H2 is neither supported nor clearly refuted. P10 (Q8) and P5 (Q10) say they would hang up, but their stated reasons are the length of the introduction and not getting a human, not learning that it is an AI.
- C3. H3 is contradicted by P4 (Q9) and P5 (Q10), as stated intentions only.
- C4. The differences between participants are better explained by context (stress, voice, introduction length, earlier experience) than by attitude to voice AI as such, and every hang-up or human-request statement is hypothetical.

## Evidence that would refute the claim, written before searching

The following list was written before the search below was run. It was written after the eight result files had been read, so it is not blind to the favoured reading. Each item is stated so that finding it would count against a named part of the claim.

- D1. A quote in which a participant compares the voice AI with an interactive voice response system or phone menu and prefers or dislikes it. This would contradict the part of C1 that says H1 cannot be tested.
- D2. A quote in which a participant says they would stay on, or did stay on, the call after learning it is an AI. This would contradict the part of C2 that says H2 has no support.
- D3. A quote in which a participant links hanging up, frustration or a request for a human to the fact that it is an AI, rather than to length, setting or a refused request. This would contradict the attribution in C2 and C4.
- D4. A quote in which the objection arises without stress, long introduction or refused request. This would contradict the context explanation in C4.
- D5. A quote reporting that someone actually hung up or actually asked for a human. This would contradict the "hypothetical only" part of C3 and C4.
- D6. A quote showing that P4 or P5 would not ask for a human at the start. This would contradict C3.
- D7. A quote from P8 or P9, or a statement of how the ten quotes were chosen. Either could overturn the reading, in either direction.
- D8. A statement in the findings that the material does not support. This would show that the favoured reading rests on something other than the quotes.

## Search used

The search covered all ten quotes (Q1 to Q10) and the eight result files in full, not a sample of them. Two passes were made. The first was a read of every quote against each item above. The second was a text search of the material for terms that would signal each item, chosen to include terms that favour the opposite of the claim: IVR, menu, press, automat, robot, stay, stayed, continue, carry on, hung, hang, human, someone, AI, intro, local, satisf, scenario, claim, accident and immediately. The terms were run against the material, and the result files were then checked for statements that go beyond what those matches show. The search is limited to this material. No transcript, scenario script or participant outside the ten quotes was available.

### D1: comparison with traditional voice automation. Not found, with one partial hit

No quote names an interactive voice response system, a phone menu or press-a-button automation. The closest is Q2, where P2 says "sometimes when you are talking to a more of a robot, it sounds more like a robot voice". That is a comparison with a robot-sounding voice, which could be a voice system. Q1 compares with chat bots, which are text tools, and Q3 refers only to the experience of having to "repeat yourself".

This does not refute C1, but it softens it. H1 is not untestable for everyone. P2 gives one indirect voice comparison, and the findings that say no quote compares at all (HE1-13, HE1-14, HE1-15) overstate the gap.

### D2: staying on the call. Partly found

No quote says "I would stay" or "I stayed". Q5 is the nearest. P4 says "at first. I mean, if it was myself, I'd be probably wanting to speak to someone, but then as the process goes on, you realise it's actually, um, a really good process." This describes someone who went through the whole process. It does not say anything about hanging up, and it is a reaction to a prerecorded scenario.

The partial hit gives C2 a small amount of support from P4 that DM-02 and CM1-05 do not record. TC1-13 rates Q5 weakly consistent with H2, which is the fairer reading. C2 should say "neither supported nor clearly refuted, with one weak supporting quote".

### D3: attribution to the fact that it is an AI. Partly found

Two quotes tie the reaction to the AI itself. In Q7, P5 says "if I was in a car accident and then immediately spoke to an AI assistant, I would be extremely frustrated" and adds "AI in its current state". The frustration is attributed to speaking to an AI assistant as well as to the stressful setting. In Q4, P10 says "this sounds very much like an AI voice". P10 does not link this to the hang-up in Q8, and the material does not say where the AI is disclosed.

This weakens the part of C2 and C4 that treats the AI disclosure as not the cause. The findings (CM1-05, HE1-13, HE1-14) say reservations are about context and delivery, "not about the AI". Q7 does not support that wording. The disclosure cannot be ruled out as a driver for P5, and for P10 the link between Q4 and Q8 is plausible but unstated.

### D4: objections that arise without stress, length or a refused request. Found

Q4 is an objection to an AI-sounding, American voice, with no stress and no mention of length. Q6 records annoyance that P1 links to "previous experiences with AI agents", with no stated scenario. Neither fits the three triggers named in the context explanation.

C4 holds for P5 (stress, voice) and for P10 in Q8 (length), but not for the whole sample. Voice character and earlier experience are separate drivers. The context explanation should be stated as several drivers and not as one.

### D5: observed behaviour. Not found

Every relevant quote is hypothetical or conditional: "I would've definitely have hung up at the intro" (Q8), "I would've asked for a human" (Q10), "If I was gonna do it, that's probably where I would do it" (Q9). Q6 reports that a human could be requested, which describes what the scenario offered. This part of C3 and C4 stands. The absence is expected, because participants heard prerecorded scenarios, so it does not strengthen the claim on its own.

### D6: P4 or P5 not asking for a human. Partly found

Q9 is hedged twice: "If I was gonna do it" and "probably". The bracketed note naming the introduction as the point is the compiler's, not P4's. Q5 says P4's wish to speak to someone applies "at first" and changes as the process goes on. For P5, Q10 gives no timing, so it does not show an immediate request. In H3, the word that matters is "immediately".

C3 should be softened. For P4, the evidence is a conditional intention that P4 says fades. For P5, it is a request with no stated timing. H3 is contradicted for two participants only in the sense that they say they might ask, and the quotes do not show that they would ask immediately. DM-02 (R3.2, "weak") is closest to this. MM-10 and TC1-13 treat P4 and P5 as plain contradictions.

### D7: P8, P9 or a selection rule. Not found

There is no quote from P8 or P9 and no account of how the ten quotes were chosen. This does not support the claim. It means the search could not test the claim against two of eight participants, and the reading may be skewed in either direction by the choice of quotes.

### D8: statements in the findings that the material does not support. Found

- Q7 is described as being about the motor claim scenario (DM-02, CM1-05, MM-10, HE1-13). The material does not say so. Q7 is hypothetical ("if I was in a car accident") and refers to "that scenario". The motor claim lodgement scenario is one of four the participants heard, so the link is a reasonable inference, but it is not recorded in the material. The context explanation for P5 leans on this inference.
- CM1-05 says the participants who object (P4, P5, P10) "all say they would ask for a human or hang up". P4 says "I'd be probably wanting to speak to someone" and, in Q9, hedges. P10 says nothing about asking for a human. Only P10 (Q8) and P5 (Q10) mention hanging up.
- HE1-14 says "No quote shows a participant who would not ask" for a human. That is true, but it counts silence from P1, P2 and P3 as absence of evidence, which is the right reading, and not as absence of a view. It should not be read as support for H3 being false in general.
- The counts differ because the files count different units. DM-02 gives four participants against three on H1, MM-10 gives four quotes against four, and HE1-15 gives three unfavourable participants. These are consistent once the unit is stated. P1 is on both sides.

## What the findings mean for the claim

The search found nothing that overturns the favoured reading. It did find four places where the reading is stated more firmly than the material allows.

- C1 survives with one adjustment. H1 remains partly supported by P1, P2 and P3. The comparison with traditional voice automation is not tested in any quote, except that Q2 offers one indirect voice comparison.
- C2 survives with one adjustment. The disclosure cannot be ruled out as a driver, because P5 names "AI in its current state" in Q7 and P10 names "an AI voice" in Q4. The reasons the findings give (introduction length, a refused human) are the stated reasons, not proven causes. P4's Q5 gives H2 weak support.
- C3 is weaker than most files state. The two participants say they would or probably would ask for a human, but the quotes give no timing for P5, hedge P4 twice, and report intentions only. "Contradicted" should read "contradicted as a stated intention, with timing unconfirmed".
- C4 is only partly borne out. Stress, introduction length and the voice are all named by participants, but Q4 and Q6 show objections that do not fit one context explanation, and the link between Q7 and the motor claim scenario is inferred.

Not finding refuting evidence for D5 and D7 is a result of the material's limits (prerecorded scenarios, six of eight participants quoted, ten selected quotes). It does not confirm the claim. For a decision, the reading is safe as a statement that this sample is split and that some participants want an early route to a human, and it is not safe as a statement about why, or about how many of the eight would act this way.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The list of refuting evidence was written after the eight result files were read. A list written before seeing the favoured reading might have included other items.
- The search covered the ten quotes and the eight result files only. The transcripts and scenario scripts would settle several items (where the AI is disclosed, which scenario each quote refers to, what P8 and P9 said) and were not available.
- Text searches were run on the quote wording, so synonyms not listed (for example "IVR" spelled out, or "press 1") could be missed. Each quote was also read in full to reduce that risk.
- The reading of tense and hedging in Q5, Q9 and Q10 is the analyst's judgement and was not checked by a second reader.
- Ten quotes from six participants cannot support a rate, a percentage or any statement beyond this sample.
- Items marked "partly found" depend on interpretation of short, informal speech with fillers and fragments.

## Alternative explanations

- The partial hits in D1 to D3 may reflect how the quotes were selected. A compiler who chose quotes to show both sides would produce near-even evidence on H1 and some ambiguity on H2 and H3 whatever participants said in full.
- The hedges in Q9 and Q10 may reflect ordinary spoken style, not real uncertainty. Read that way, P4 and P5 would ask for a human early, and the original reading of C3 stands.
- P5 may be reacting to the scenario (a car accident) as the findings say, and the words "AI in its current state" may be incidental. Read that way, D3 does not weaken C2.
- Participants may overstate hypothetical reactions to prerecorded scenarios. This would weaken the contradiction of H2 and H3 further, and it would not support the hypotheses either.
- The favourable group (P1, P2, P3) and the unfavourable group (P4, P5, P10) may differ in the question they were answering, not in their feelings. P4 and P1 each hold both views, which supports this reading.
