# Framework matrix of eight interviews on voice AI customer support

## Themes (columns)

The columns follow the three hypotheses, plus one theme that emerged from the quotes.

- T1, satisfaction and comparison with earlier automation (H1)
- T2, hanging up on learning it is an AI (H2)
- T3, asking for a human agent (H3)
- T4, voice and naturalness (emerged from the quotes)

## Matrix

Each cell summarises only the quotes shown in the material. "No coded data" means the material has no quote that fits the theme for that participant. It does not mean the participant held no view.

## Empty cells

- P8 and P9 have no coded data in any column. The material gives no quote from either, so the matrix cannot place them.
- T2 is empty for P1, P2, P3, P4, P8 and P9. Only P5 and P10 have anything coded for hanging up.
- T3 is empty for P2, P3, P8, P9 and P10. P1's cell holds a mention of the human option, not a request for one.
- T1 has no direct comparison with traditional voice automation in any cell. The comparators in the quotes are chat bots (P1), a "robot voice" (P2), having to repeat yourself (P3) and AI agents (P1).

## Reading across the rows (cases)

- P1 is mixed on its own. Q1 is positive about this AI, while Q6 records annoyance linked to earlier experiences and the human-agent option.
- P2 and P3 are the only rows that are positive across all their coded cells, and both mention that it felt like a real person. Neither has any data on hanging up or asking for a human.
- P4 starts hesitant and ends positive, and holds both a want to speak to someone (Q5) and a possible request for a human at the introduction (Q9).
- P5 and P10 are the rows with hang-up data. In both, the stated trigger is something other than the bare fact of speaking to an AI: a failed request for a human (P5, Q10) and the length of the introduction (P10, Q8).
- P8 and P9 contribute nothing.

## Reading down the columns (themes)

- T1 (H1): the quoted positive reactions (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5) are all comparisons with something other than traditional voice automation, or are within-scenario judgements. The material cannot show that participants found this more satisfying than traditional voice automation. The negative quotes (Q4, Q6, Q7) are about context, voice or earlier experience.
- T2 (H2): the only quotes about hanging up (Q8, Q10) describe hanging up as something P10 would have done and P5 might have done. No quote shows a participant continuing after learning it is an AI, apart from P4's account of moving from hesitation to approval (Q5), which is not about hanging up. The material does not support H2 and has one direct counterexample (Q8).
- T3 (H3): two participants say they would, or probably would, ask for a human (P4, P5). No quote shows a participant who would not ask. Q5 and Q9 describe an initial impulse that P4 says passed. The material does not support H3, and it leans against it for P4 and P5.
- T4 (emerged): the voice is mentioned in four rows and splits by direction. P2 and P3 read it as human-like, and P5 and P10 read it as cold, clinical or too obviously AI.

## What the matrix shows and does not show

Within this sample, the quotes link hanging up and asking for a human to the scenario and the introduction (Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10), and link positive reactions to the voice sounding human-like (Q2, Q3). The matrix does not show how many of the eight participants hold each view. The cells are coded summaries of selected quotes, so the number of filled cells must not be read as a count of responses. For a decision, treat H1 as untested against its stated comparator, H2 as not supported with one contrary quote, and H3 as not supported, with two participants who lean against it.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The technique assumed that the ten quotes are a fair record of each participant's views. The material says only some participants are quoted and gives no basis for how they were chosen, so the matrix may under-represent views.
- It assumed that the quotes can be coded against themes without the full transcripts. Several cells rest on a single quote, and Q9 and Q10 are hypothetical statements about what the participant would have done.
- The technique is built for an experienced qualitative lead working from full coded transcripts. With ten quotes it works as an audit trail of what is and is not in the material, not as a full framework analysis.
- Q9 is a short fragment whose meaning depends on the bracketed note. Q6 is ambiguous about whether P1 would ask for a human.
- The material does not say which of the four scenarios each quote refers to, apart from the motor claim reference in Q7. Differences between scenarios cannot be separated from differences between people.
- T4 was added by the analyst from the quotes. It is not one of the hypotheses.

## Alternative explanations

- The pattern in T2 and T3 may reflect scenario stress rather than a general attitude to AI. P5's reactions (Q7, Q10) are tied to a car accident, and P10's hang-up (Q8) is tied to the length of the introduction. Under this reading, hanging up and asking for a human depend on the situation and the introduction design, and the hypotheses H2 and H3 are too broad to test as stated.
- The positive T1 quotes may reflect the voice quality of the prerecorded scenarios rather than satisfaction with voice AI in general. P2 and P3 both praise how human it sounded, while P5 and P10 object to the voice, so the voice may be the driver in both directions.
- The empty T2 and T3 cells for most participants may reflect what the interviewer asked or what was selected for quoting, not an absence of views. A different selection of quotes could change the reading of H2 and H3.
