# Hypothesis tree: do the interviews support H1 to H3?

## Tree

Each node is a claim that must be true for its parent to be true. Evidence status is given for each leaf, using only Q1 to Q10 and participants P1 to P5 and P10. Statements are patterns within this sample only.

**R. The eight interviews support H1, H2 and H3.**
Reading: the root fails as stated. H1 has a favourable direction but cannot be tested as a comparison. H2 is neither supported nor refuted. H3 is contradicted by two quoted participants.

- **R1. H1: customers find voice AI interactions more satisfying than traditional voice automation.**
  - **R1.1 Participants express satisfaction with the voice AI scenarios.**
    - **R1.1a More quoted participants are favourable than unfavourable.**
      Status: mixed, leaning favourable. Favourable at least once: P1 (Q1, "this one seems to be pretty dynamic"), P2 (Q2, "it knew exactly what it needed to ask"), P3 (Q3, "really seamless") and P4 (Q5, "a really good process", reached only after initial reluctance). Unfavourable at least once: P1 (Q6, "I felt a little bit annoyed"), P5 (Q7, "I would be extremely frustrated"; Q7 is about a car accident scenario) and P10 (Q4, "it's American"; Q8, "it was way too long"). That is four participants against three, with P1 on both sides.
  - **R1.2 Participants judged the AI against earlier automated systems.**
    - **R1.2a Participants contrast the AI with earlier automation.**
      Status: supported for three participants. P1 (Q1, "those never work"), P2 (Q2, "sometimes when you are talking to a more of a robot, it sounds more like a robot voice") and P3 (Q3, "you have to repeat yourself").
    - **R1.2b The earlier automation they name is voice automation.**
      Status: partly supported. Only P2 (Q2) clearly refers to a voice. P1 (Q1) refers to chat bots that "parsed, um, articles from their own website", which is not voice automation. P3 (Q3) does not say what the earlier system was.
  - **R1.3 Satisfaction with voice AI and with traditional voice automation was assessed on the same basis for the same participants.**
    Status: no evidence possible (see list below).

- **R2. H2: customers do not hang up immediately when they learn they are speaking with an AI.**
  - **R2.1 Participants learn they are speaking with an AI at an identifiable point.**
    Status: no evidence possible. Q4 shows P10 recognising "an AI voice", but the material does not say whether the scenarios disclosed the AI or at what point.
  - **R2.2 Participants do not say they would end the call straight away.**
    Status: mixed. Against: P10 (Q8, "I would've definitely have hung up at the intro."). Conditional only: P5 (Q10, "I probably would've hung up and then tried to do it manually online", said after not getting a human). No such statement from P1, P2, P3 or P4, but silence is not evidence that they would stay. P4 (Q5) says the process becomes good as it goes on.
  - **R2.3 Any hang-up statement is caused by learning it is an AI, not by something else.**
    Status: unresolved. In Q8 P10 gives the length of the introduction as the reason ("it was way too long"). The same participant objects to the AI voice and its American accent in Q4. The material does not link either objection to the hang-up.
  - **R2.4 Stated intentions to hang up or stay reflect what participants would do in a live call.**
    Status: no evidence possible (see list below).

- **R3. H3: customers do not immediately ask for a human agent.**
  - **R3.1 Participants do not say they would ask for a human at the start.**
    Status: against, for two participants. P4 (Q9, "If I was gonna do it, that's probably where I would do it", about the introduction) and P5 (Q10, "I would've asked for a human."). P4 also says in Q5 "I'd be probably wanting to speak to someone" at first. P1 (Q6) mentions that a human could be requested at the beginning but does not say they would ask. P2 and P3 say nothing on the topic.
  - **R3.2 The point at which a request would be made is later than "immediately".**
    Status: weak. Only P4 names a point, the introduction (Q9), which is the earliest point in the scenario. P5 gives no timing in Q10, although Q7 describes a reaction to speaking to an AI assistant "immediately" in a car accident.
  - **R3.3 Stated intentions to ask for a human reflect what participants would do in a live call.**
    Status: no evidence possible (see list below).

- **R4. The quoted material represents all eight interviews.**
  - **R4.1 Every participant's view is available.**
    Status: no evidence possible (see list below).

## Leaf claims for which no evidence is possible

- **R1.3** Satisfaction with voice AI and with traditional voice automation assessed on the same basis for the same participants. The material describes four prerecorded voice AI scenarios and no traditional automation scenario, and it contains no satisfaction ratings. Comparisons in Q1 to Q3 are from memory of earlier experiences.
- **R2.1** Participants learn they are speaking with an AI at an identifiable point. The material does not say whether or when the scenarios disclosed the AI.
- **R2.4 and R3.3** Stated intentions reflect live-call behaviour. Participants listened to prerecorded scenarios, so no one hung up or asked for a human. Every relevant statement is hypothetical ("would've", "if I was").
- **R4.1** Every participant's view is available. P8 and P9 have no quote, and the material says only that some participants are quoted. The basis for choosing the ten quotes is not given.

## Assumptions and limitations

The technique assumed that the three hypotheses can be split into sub-claims that are each necessary, and that interview statements count as evidence for each leaf. It cannot weigh leaves against each other, so the overall reading rests on judgement.

Six of eight participants are quoted, and the quotes are a selection of unknown basis. Counts such as four against three are counts of quoted participants, not of the sample. Silence in a quote is treated as no evidence, not as support. The tree can show which claims lack evidence but cannot supply it, and it cannot check the transcripts or scenario scripts, which would settle R2.1 and R1.2b.

## Alternative explanations

The split between participants may reflect context, not attitude to voice AI. The two strongest statements about hanging up or asking for a human come from P5 about a car accident (Q7, Q10) and P10 about an introduction judged too long (Q8). Stress and introduction length, not the AI itself, could be driving them. This would leave H2 and H3 undecided for ordinary calls.

The pattern could also be an artefact of listening to recordings. Participants who rated the AI favourably (P1, P2, P3) did not discuss hanging up or asking for a human, while those who did (P4, P5, P10) were answering what they would do. The favourable and unfavourable groups may differ in the question being answered, not in how they feel. P4 shows the two can coexist: Q5 calls the process "a really good process" while Q9 places a request for a human at the introduction.
