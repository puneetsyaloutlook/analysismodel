# Content analysis of the ten quotes against H1 to H3

The quotes support none of the three hypotheses cleanly. Favourable reactions (H1) appear in three of the six quoted participants. Statements that contradict H2 and H3 appear for P10, P4 and P5. Every H2 and H3 statement is hypothetical ("would"), so no observed behaviour is available. Counts below are within this sample only. Two participants (P8, P9) have no quote in the material.

## Coder agreement

Not available. One coder applied the scheme once, so no agreement statistic can be reported. Categories C, D and G required judgement (Q6 and Q4 especially), and an independent second coding would be needed before treating these counts as firm.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The scheme was built from the three hypotheses and the quotes themselves, not from a pre-existing codebook. Categories that fit H1 to H3 were favoured over other themes the quotes might hold.
- Counts describe the ten selected quotes only. The material says only some participants are quoted and that the order is arbitrary, so counts cannot show how often a view occurs among the eight interviewees. P8 and P9 are not represented at all.
- The quotes are extracts. Content analysis removes the surrounding context, and the material does not give the question that prompted each quote, whether participants were told they were speaking with an AI, or which of the four scenarios each quote refers to.
- Most H2 and H3 evidence is conditional or hypothetical speech ("would"). Categories E and F measure stated intention, not behaviour.
- H1 compares voice AI with traditional voice automation, but no quote rates satisfaction directly or compares the two voice systems. Category A is the closest available proxy.
- A single coder was used, so no agreement check was possible.

## Alternative explanations

- The favourable reactions (A, B) may reflect the scenarios chosen or the quality of the specific prerecorded voice, not voice AI in general. The unfavourable ones (C, E, F) may reflect the stressful claim scenario (Q7) or the introduction's length (Q8), not the AI itself.
- The unequal counts may reflect who was quoted. If the quoted set favoured vivid reactions, categories C, E and F could be over-represented relative to the full transcripts, and favourable but unremarkable comments could be under-represented.
- The apparent split may be about individual prior experience, not the technology. P1 and P4 refer to earlier experiences or first impressions that shaped their reaction, so earlier exposure to automation could explain more than the AI voice does.
