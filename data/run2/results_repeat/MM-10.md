# Stance detection of ten quotes toward H1, H2 and H3

## Targets and labels

Each hypothesis is treated as a named target, stated as a proposition. A label of "favour" means the quote supports the proposition, "oppose" means it contradicts it, "neutral" means the quote addresses the target but takes mixed or conditional ground, and "none" means the quote does not address the target.

- H1: voice AI interactions are more satisfying than traditional voice automation.
- H2: customers do not hang up immediately when they learn they are speaking with an AI.
- H3: customers do not immediately ask for a human agent.

Labels cover only what each quote says. Where a quote reports what the AI said or what a participant imagines doing, the label follows the participant's own position, not the reported speech.

## What the labels show

Within this sample of quoted participants, the quotes link to the hypotheses as follows.

- H1 has the most favourable labels (Q1, Q2, Q3, from three participants), and one opposing label (Q7, P5). The favourable quotes compare the AI with chatbots, robot voices and repeating yourself, not directly with traditional voice automation, so even the favour labels fit H1 only partly.
- H2 has no favourable label. Q8 opposes it and Q10 is neutral.
- H3 has no favourable label. Q9 and Q10 oppose it, and Q5 is neutral.

For a decision, this means the interviews offer some support for H1 and no support for H2 or H3. The evidence on H2 and H3 points against them, but it is hypothetical in all cases ("would've", "if I was").

## Assumptions and limitations

- The technique needs an explicit, defined target and normally 100 or more texts that mention it. This material has ten quotes and three targets, so the counts are descriptive only and cannot be read as shares of customers.
- The targets are hypotheses, not named policies or people. Each had to be restated as a proposition, and the labels depend on that wording. "Favour" here means supports the hypothesis, which is not always the same as the participant liking the AI.
- Quotes were selected for the material and only six of eight participants are quoted. The labels cannot show the stance of P8 and P9, or of the unquoted parts of any interview.
- Time and scenario cannot be analysed, because the material provides neither.
- Most H2 and H3 quotes describe imagined behaviour, not behaviour observed during the prerecorded scenarios. Stance detection labels what was said, not what was done.
- The number of "none" labels depends on how strictly a quote has to mention the target, and a looser reading would move some rows to favour or oppose.

## Alternative explanations

- The favourable H1 labels may reflect the participants' comparison with chatbots and robot voices, not with traditional voice automation. On this reading Q1, Q2 and Q3 show that the AI sounded human, not that it was more satisfying than earlier voice automation.
- The H2 and H3 opposing labels may reflect the scenario, not the AI. Q7 refers to a car accident, and Q10 and Q8 relate to the introduction, which P10 calls "way too long". The cause could be the length and tone of the introduction, or the stressful situation, rather than the fact that the caller is an AI.
- The quotes may have been picked because they were vivid, which would overstate both support and opposition. The absence of favourable H2 and H3 quotes may only reflect that participants who did not hang up or ask for a human had nothing to say about it.
